# Program language model: working notes

This directory holds the code, the run records and the development data of the experiments on the program language model (LM) and its feature encoder. No ticket covers them. The exports, the venv, the Kaggle kernels and the weights are under `work/lm/` and `work/tables/`, which git ignores. The encoder's design is in [feature-encoder.md](../docs/feature-encoder.md). Read that design in full before you propose encoder work. It settles much that can look open, and its Open section lists what is not settled.

## Purpose

The LM writes arclog programs token by token under the engine's token mask, and the engine verifies each program on the train pairs. The LM alone never sees the task, so it can only learn which programs are plausible, and it solves almost no tasks. The encoder is meant to give it the tables that each clause computes. The LM experiments measure three things:
- whether the corpus programs teach the language, by held-out loss for each token class;
- whether the pointer reads work, by pointer accuracy;
- the smallest base model that works.

## Data

- [export.py](export.py) gets every case's token stream from the `arclog` package (`arclog.corpus_streams`), one call per task in worker processes, and writes `work/lm/data/cases.npz` and `work/lm/data/vocab.json`, which holds each token's name and kind. Its docstring gives the layout. `cases.npz` holds the engine's mask at each model-written step, as a table of distinct masks and an index into it for each step.
    - A stream is dropped when its model reads a relation or label before the engine declares it, or when the engine gives it no masks; the stream report gives the reason. Identical streams of one task are kept once.
    - A task is marked held out when its id is in `work/lm/data/evaluation.ids`, the public evaluation set.
- Before you export, rebuild the CLI and the package, or they read a vocabulary that differs from the engine's:
    - `cargo build --profile tool -p arclog-cli` in `engine/`;
    - `VIRTUAL_ENV=work/lm/.venv CARGO_TARGET_DIR=work/lm/target uvx maturin develop --release` in `engine/arclog-py`, with absolute paths.
- Cases are the cross product of a solver's choice points, so a few tasks have thousands of cases and most have one to four. Training draws one case per task each epoch, so each task has the same weight.
- `train.py` splits the tasks three ways, with a fixed seed, and asserts that the sets share no task and no family:
    - **training**: the tasks not held out, less the in-distribution set;
    - **in-distribution**: `--indist` tasks drawn at random from the training tasks, whole families together. It selects the checkpoint.
    - `--fold k/n` (default none) restricts the training tasks to those whose task id has a SHA-1 prefix (8 hex digits) that is k modulo n, and leaves the in-distribution and evaluation sets as they are; the data line and `model.json` give the fold.
    - **evaluation**: the held-out tasks, less any whose family has a task in the other two sets. A family is a solver and the siblings whose `@representative` header names it, read from `programs/solvers/*.dl`.
    - On the current data: 766 training tasks, 80 in-distribution, 92 evaluation, with 1818057f dropped as the representative of the training task 7e02026e.
- The evaluation programs are about twice as long as the training programs, and the evaluation tasks are harder. Evaluation loss therefore mixes generalization with the shift to harder tasks; the in-distribution loss separates the two. Use both to compare runs and to see a trend, and neither as an absolute measure.

## Training

- [train.py](train.py) fine-tunes a pretrained decoder. Its docstring gives the model.
    - Relations and labels take a random identifier for each training example. A read is predicted as a class, then by a pointer over the declarations made so far.
    - The loss, in training and in evaluation, counts only the choices the solver's decode makes: each model-written step at which the engine's mask leaves more than one option. At such a step the class distribution is over the out tokens the mask admits, and the pointer distribution is over the admitted declarations that are the latest of their identifier. A step with one option adds nothing to the loss, the count or the stats.
    - Vocabulary embeddings start from the mean of the base model's embeddings of each token's name.
    - Weights and optimizer state are fp32. The forward pass runs under bf16 autocast on CUDA. `--pure-bf16` gives the setup that size runs 1–3 used: bf16 weights and optimizer state, with no fp32 copy.
    - Each evaluation logs the in-distribution loss under `indist` and the evaluation loss under `heldout`, each with its loss by token class and pointer accuracy, to `--out/log.jsonl`. `--eval-train` adds the training programs under `train`. `train_loss` is the language model loss plus `--aux-masked` times the masked table loss. With `--aux-masked`, `lm_loss` and `masked_loss` give the two parts, and `grad_norm_masked` and `grad_norm_total` give the global gradient norm after the masked loss's backward and after both backwards, before clipping, on the steps the step line prints. `--aux-clip <norm>` (default 1.0) scales the gradient of the masked loss, right after its backward, so that its global norm is at most that norm, and the step line gives `grad_norm_masked_clipped`, the norm after the scaling, on the steps where it applied. The checkpoint of lowest in-distribution loss is saved to `--out` as `model.pt` and `model.json`.
    - A program longer than `--budget` forms a batch alone, so `--max-len` bounds the peak memory, and `--budget` does not. The longest stream is 3,540 steps; `--max-len 2048` truncates about 4% of the training cases and 2 of the 93 evaluation programs.
- Code check: `--max-steps 2 --train-limit 8 --eval-limit 4 --eval-train --budget 1024 --max-len 1024 --device cpu`. It runs the split's assertions on the full data.
- The Mac's GPU (`--device mps`) compiles a kernel for each new tensor shape, so the encoder's varying table shapes make it unusable for runs with `--tables`: the first pretrain step took 175 s. Dev checks stay on the CPU. Without the encoder, SmolLM2-135M in fp32 at `--budget 1024 --max-len 2048` ran at 0.82 s per step, about 5 minutes an epoch, with 14.6 GB of driver-allocated memory at peak.
- [sample.py](sample.py) samples programs from a `train.py` run directory under the engine's token mask, with the class and pointer choices that the loss counts, and writes each program's id stream, its end and its last consistency as one JSON line; a program ends at the first `OUT` form that holds. With `--prefix`, each program starts from a prefix of one of the task's corpus programs. `--max-clause <n>` ends a program that writes more than n model-written tokens inside one clause (default 1.5 times the largest count in `--data`'s cases, printed at start; 0 for none), and `--max-steps-factor <f>` lowers `--max-steps` to f times the source case's model-written tokens (or the task's median from nothing), and `--max-cont-factor <f>` ends a prefix-mode program ("max-cont") at f times the model-written tokens that the source case has after the prefix. `--rule-tests` turns on the decode setting `rule_tests`, so that each `RULE` clause's end gives the verdict of the test of its relation, which the lines' `rules` hold; it is off by default. It writes the decode settings of its sessions beside the lines, for a replay of the streams. Its docstring gives the fields. It does not run the feature encoder.

The pointer path has no bug: on a synthetic table where Color is a function of a visible Number, the pointer loss falls from chance to near zero in 200 steps on the CPU. On real tables it has no cue: a visible column of the same row picks out the same-valued rows for only about 29% of hidden Color fields, and a computed Color's value comes from a source table that the row sees only as that table's K pooled vectors, so the hidden row cannot look up its source row and flat scores, which are exactly chance, are the optimum. The orientation heads at ln 4 and ln 2 are the same picture. The pointer learns only if the hidden field's source rows reach it as attention keys.

## Encoder

- [encoder.py](encoder.py) holds the feature encoder of [feature-encoder.md](../docs/feature-encoder.md).
- `train.py --tables <dir>` reads a tables export and inserts K feature vectors per table after the token whose step gives that table. `--enc-k 0` with `--tables` runs the baseline on the same case set.
- The encoder flags are `--enc-k`, `--enc-layers`, `--enc-width`, `--enc-heads`, `--enc-mlp`, `--enc-gram`, `--enc-budget`, `--enc-max-rows`, `--enc-source-rows` and `--enc-ptr-equality`.
- `--enc-checkpoint`, in `train.py` and `pretrain.py`, recomputes the activations of each encoder layer in the backward pass instead of keeping them, which saves device memory and costs time. It is on by default with cuda and off otherwise. A step of full-data programs fits device memory with it alone, since without it each table keeps its evidence rows' keys and values at every layer for the backward pass. A checkpointed layer takes its batch's evidence keys and values from the shared ones by one gathered index, and keeps only that index for its backward pass. A batch of fewer keys than `CHECKPOINT_MIN` in [encoder.py](encoder.py), B (N + P + E), runs without the checkpoint, and keeps at most about 0.1 GB more at the default shape.
- `pretrain.py --step-rows <n>` caps a step's programs by their summed cost in rows (`step_cost`), at most `--programs` of them and at least one.
- `train.py --enc-group <n>` encodes the programs of a batch n at a time, one encoder call per group, and 0 encodes them all in one call. The batches themselves do not change. The default is 0. With n above 0, the encoder's identifiers are drawn in another order than with 0, from the same distribution.
- `pretrain.py --autocast` runs the encoder under bf16 autocast with fp32 weights, as `train.py` does with cuda. It is on by default with cuda and off otherwise.
- `--enc-equality`, in `train.py` and `pretrain.py`, chooses how the equality term reaches the attention scores of a table's rows.
    - `codes`, the default, adds each field's code to the queries and the keys: a one-hot for a Color, an Example, an orientation constant and each margin of a Box, and a random unit vector for a Number and an Ident.
    - `random` adds a random sign code of 32 places for each value, drawn for each decode as the identifiers are. An equal pair of values scores the learned weight, and an unequal pair about the weight over the square root of 32, of either sign.
    - `flex` adds the learned weight where two rows' values are equal, exactly and with no extra width, through `flex_attention`. It trains on cuda alone, since `flex_attention` has no backward pass on the CPU.
- With `--aux-masked`, the backward pass of the masked table loss runs before the clean encoder pass, one program group at a time, so that its graph is freed first.
- The encoder plans every level of the call first and builds the row tokens and the equality codes of all levels at once, the codes of each level in its own layout; it then runs one level at a time, the t-th table of every program of the call, with the level's tables cut into batches by `encoder.batches`. `BATCH_COST` in [encoder.py](encoder.py) sets how much padding one fewer batch is worth.
- At each layer, a table's rows attend to the rows of the earlier evidence tables (`ex_input`, `ex_output`) after that layer, as well as to the earlier tables' pooled vectors.
- `--enc-source-rows`, in `train.py` and `pretrain.py`, off by default: a derived table's rows also attend, at each layer, to the rows of its source tables after that layer (`encoder.source_tables`: the earlier derived tables that its columns' flows name, one level deep), with an equality term of each column against the source columns its flows name, under the weights `src_eq`. A source table's keys and values at each layer are computed once, when its level runs, and kept for the later levels (`SourceRows`); a table over `--enc-max-rows` gives none. It needs the export's column-level provenance and does not run with `--enc-equality flex`. `pretrain.py`'s `step_cost` counts a source table's rows at `SOURCE_ROW_COST` and each source key a table reads at `SOURCE_KEY_COST`.
- `--enc-ptr-equality`, in `train.py` and `pretrain.py`, off by default: the pointer heads take equality terms (`masked_fields`), a weight per pair of the hidden column and a visible column of the same type in the hidden row (`ptr_eq`), and with `--enc-source-rows`, a weight per head (`ptr_src`) on the inner product of a candidate's code with the codes that the hidden row's last layer gathers from its source keys. The source equality weights and these enter times `SOURCE_EQ_GAIN` and `POINTER_EQ_GAIN`. An `--encoder-init` state without these weights leaves them at 0.
- An entry the encoder cannot see, or one over `--enc-budget`, enters its table as one marker row. A table enters as unseen only when its projection search stopped, it faults, every entry is unseen, or it holds more rows than `--enc-max-rows`, summed over its entries. That flag is a guard on device memory and on step time, 4000 by default; the evidence tables are exempt. A table whose entries hold several assignments enters as one table per assignment, and the flag applies to each. The unseen marker is a fixed vector, not learned.
- [pretrain.py](pretrain.py) trains the encoder alone on the masked table loss and saves it for `train.py --encoder-init <file>`. `train.py --aux-masked <weight>` adds the masked table loss beside the next-token loss. `--eval-limit` sets how many held-out programs the evaluation uses, a sample drawn once with `--seed`, and 0 takes all of them. `--mask-frac` and `--mask-cap` set the fields it hides per table. `--mask-kind` sets each program's step kind: a program step hides computed fields, an `output` step hides the computed fields of `ex_output` alone, a prior step hides inferrable fields, and `alternate`, the default, takes the three by turns. `--prior-weight` weighs the prior part of the loss beside its program part. A program step hides a computed field only where a source of its column is in its own table or in `ex_input` or `ex_output`, which its row sees row by row. A field is `remote` when its column has no source in its own table or in `ex_input` or `ex_output`, because its sources are all in other derived tables or its flows name no table or only its own export table: it stays visible, and `--mask-remote` (off by default) hides it too, one field at a time. `--mask-remote-groups` (off by default; it stops the run when given with `--mask-remote`) hides remote fields only in the columns of `encoder.group_columns`, those with a source pair whose source tables are all seen, by key group (`encoder.key_groups`): one draw per group, at `--mask-frac`, hides the group's column in all its rows (`FeatureEncoder.group_units`). A hidden group counts its fields toward `--mask-cap`, and where a table's units hold more than the cap, a group that does not fit stays visible whole. It also stops the run when given with `--enc-source-rows`. Under it, the preflight gives a second line: the hidden remote fields by type in one draw per program, for the pointer types the share whose target is "no row holds it", and the pointer-type fields with a row target, apart from those with a cross-entry row target (below); it stops the run when no pointer-type remote field has a row target other than a cross-entry one. With either flag, a remote field is labelled `remote/<type>` in `by_type` and is left out of `by_depth`, and the pretrain log gives `by_source_rows`: per bucket of the most rows of a source table of the field's column (1-50, 51-200, 201-1000, 1001-4000), per type, the loss, chance level, accuracy and fields, with `group_loss` and `group_chance_loss`, the means over key groups of each group's mean loss and mean chance loss, and `groups`; a pointer type's entry also splits its fields into `row_target`, `row_target_cross_entry` (a visible row of another entry agrees with the hidden row on every key column of its group and holds the value, `encoder.remote_fields`) and `no_row_target`. `remote` gives the same over all remote fields. With `--mask-remote-groups`, the held-out log of `pretrain.py` gives `parent_attention`: per layer (`layers`) and over the layers (`mean`), the mean over hidden remote fields and heads of the share of the field's row's attention over the earlier tables' pooled vectors that lands on the pooled vectors of its column's source tables, beside `chance`, the share under equal scores, and `fields`; and over the `matched_fields`, those whose row attends to at least as many earlier tables that are not its sources as source tables, `matched_parent` and `matched_recent` (per layer `matched_parent_layers` and `matched_recent_layers`), the share on its source tables and the share on as many of the latest other earlier tables; and over the `matched_nondirect_fields`, those whose row attends to at least as many earlier tables that are no source table of any column of its table as source tables, `matched_recent_nondirect`, the share on as many of the latest of those, and `matched_parent_nondirect`, the share on its source tables (`encoder.FeatureEncoder.parent_shares`). The probe reads the attention scores at evaluation and changes no computed value. `--enc-parent-bias B` (0, off, by default) adds to a row's attention scores against an earlier table's pooled vectors a learned value per layer by the parent type of that table to the row's table (`encoder.parent_types`: direct source, ancestor, unrelated), which starts at [B, 0, 0]; with it, the held-out log gives `parent_bias`, the per-layer values under `direct`, `ancestor` and `unrelated`, and the probe measures the attention with the bias in it. It refuses `--enc-equality flex`, and an encoder pretrained with it loads into `train.py --encoder-init` only with the flag. An export without the column-level provenance keeps the table-level rule, which has no `remote` label. The Training section of the design gives the labels and the rule. The loader reads the labels and sources from the export's `flows.parquet` and the `depth`, `keys`, `output_position` and `output_color` columns of `tables.parquet`. For an export without them, it takes the table-level rule, and its stats line says which it took. A cache build stops with a DataFault where a flow, a key or an output column names a column its table does not hold. Before the model loads, `train.py` and `pretrain.py` run `encoder.preflight`, the labels, the sources and one mask draw per step kind on every program, and stop at the first program it cannot mask; `--no-preflight` skips it. The preflight line gives the number of fields of each label. The pretrain log gives the loss of each part, and the loss and the accuracy by type per part; `chance_loss`, the mean loss of a head with equal scores over the same fields, is logged beside the `loss` of each type and each depth, and as `program_chance_loss` and `prior_chance_loss` beside the parts' losses. It counts a Box or Position field right when all four margins are, and gives `edge_accuracy`, the fraction of single margins right, beside it.
- A hidden Color, Example or Ident field is predicted as a pointer to a row of its own table that shows the same value, or as "no row holds it"; its chance loss is -log of the share of such rows among the candidates and that class, and the log gives it beside the loss. A computed field's value comes from a row of a source table, which the hidden row reaches as keys only with `--enc-source-rows`, and the pointer compares random identifiers only through the equality terms of `--enc-ptr-equality`; on the synthetic cases of `encoder_check.py`, the pointer learns with the flags and stays at its chance level without them.
- A Box or Position loss is the mean over its four margins of a 320-way class, so its chance level is ln 320; `edge_accuracy` is one margin right and `accuracy` all four.
- A rollout export is a tables export in the loader's layout of the continuations that the baseline LM samples from the branch points of the gold programs, a branch point being a rule boundary. Its case names are `<task>__<case>__b<j>__r<k>` for the k-th continuation sampled at branch point j (a rule index) of corpus case `<case>`, `<task>__<case>__b<j>__gold` for the gold continuation and `<task>__none__b0__r<k>` for a sample from nothing (`sample.py --prefix 0`); an empty prefix of a corpus case is its branch point 0. A case's tables, as the loader reads them, are the gold prefix's, then the continuation's. Its `report.txt` gives, per case, `<task> <case>: ended <kind> at token <i>` and any fault line.
- [export_rollouts.py](export_rollouts.py) writes a rollout export from one or more of the sampler's JSON lines files, one part per task. Its docstring gives the checks it makes of the prefix tables and what it does where a task's replay panics.
- `labels.jsonl`, in the rollout export's directory, gives one JSON line per case: `case`, `task`, `corpus_case` (null from nothing), `branch`, `rollout` (k or `"gold"`), `prefix_tokens`, `prefix_rules`, `prefix_tables`, `ended` (the sampler's), `consistency` (the report's verdict, or null), `solved_test`, `fault` (the report's fault line or null), `sampler_run` and `sampler_fold` (the sampling LM's run directory and its `--fold`, null for the gold continuation), `heldout` (whether the sampler's `--data` export marks the task held out), `rules` (the sampler's: the rule test's verdict on each rule of the continuation, in order, null where the sampler's settings line does not give `rule_tests` true) and `rule_ends` (the stream index of each rule's end). The first table after the branch is export table `prefix_tables`. [rollout_labels.py](rollout_labels.py) writes the file from the sampler's JSON lines and the export, and its docstring says how it counts `prefix_tables` and which sampler lines it refuses.
- `encoder.load_rollouts` reads a rollout export with its labels. Each table gets a value record: its group (task, corpus case, `prefix_tokens`), its member (the rollout), its two labels, its position after the branch (0 for the first table, negative for the prefix's tables), and the flags empty, unseen, faulted and unjudged. The solving label is whether the member is solving (one with no `fault` that is the gold continuation or ends `Consistent` with `solved_test` true). The rule test's verdict on a RULE clause is whether the relation that its END finishes, read as the output, solves every train pair; so the consistent label, the verdict on the rule whose evaluation gave the table (1 for `Consistent`), marks the tables of a rule whose relation is the output. It needs a rollout export of a sampler run with `--rule-tests`, and `--value-label consistent` refuses an export made with them off. A table of the prefix, or of a last rule with no verdict, has none and is unjudged. The tables come through `load_tables`, with a cache of their own beside the data's. It raises a DataFault where a sampled case's `sampler_fold` k/n holds its task (`train.fold_of(task, n) == k`) and its `heldout` is not true: a fold LM trains on its fold's tasks less the held-out ones.
- `pretrain.py --value <mode>` trains a value head (`encoder.ValueHead`, one logit per table from the encoder's `Readout` of its K pooled vectors) on the rollout export of `--value-tables`, beside the masked loss, times `--value-weight` (default 1.0). `ranking` is a listwise softmax cross-entropy over each branch point's continuations, and `binary` a sigmoid cross-entropy on each continuation's score, its mean logit at the position, with its own label; `off` is the default. The loss takes the tables at the positions below `--value-positions` (default 1), each position its own list, and leaves out empty, unseen and faulted tables, under `--value-label consistent` (`solving`, the default, takes the solving label, which marks every table of a continuation that solves or is the gold continuation) unjudged tables, and, with `ranking`, the lists whose members' labels do not differ. `--value-groups` (default 1) sets the branch points per step, each with all its continuations, within `--step-rows`. A table's logit depends on the draw of the random identifiers of its relations and values, so the held-out metrics take each table's mean logit over `--value-draws` draws (default 1) and give each metric's least and greatest value over the draws taken one at a time. The docstring of `pretrain.py` gives the step and the log fields `value_loss`, `value_pair_acc`, `value_pair_acc_sampled`, `value_auc` and `value_auc_sampled`.
- [value_eval.py](value_eval.py) measures a value head that `pretrain.py --value` saved on the held-out branch points of a rollout export, by pairwise ranking accuracy and AUC, beside a logistic regression on trivial table features and the masked loss per field as a score. The head's score is its mean logit over `--draws` draws of identifiers (default 4). Its metrics come from the function that gives `pretrain.py`'s held-out ones (`encoder.value_draw_metrics`). Its docstring gives the three scores.
- [value_probe.py](value_probe.py) fits ridge regressions from the value head's input, the encoder's readout of a table, on the training branch points of a rollout export, and scores them on the held-out ones: to the solving label, to each trivial feature of value_eval.py, and beside value_eval.py's logistic regression with and without the position and the step index. It runs on a trained encoder.pt or on a random initialization of pretrain.py's default shape. Its docstring gives the probe targets and the flags that cut the input for a quick run.
- [encoder_check.py](encoder_check.py) is the symmetry check of the encoder and the masked loss heads, and the check of the field labels, the masks and the loss parts; the checks of the flex equality mode run only with `--flex`, and the two pointer learnability runs (200 training steps each) only with `--learn`. [insertion_check.py](insertion_check.py) is the sequence-insertion check. [value_check.py](value_check.py) checks the value head's labels, records, lists and losses on a synthetic rollout export, and its learning run only with `--learn`. [regression_check.py](regression_check.py) checks that `train.py` without `--tables` trains as a reference copy does. [checks.sh](checks.sh) runs the correctness checks at once, with a portability check of the files that go to the pod, and gives PASS or FAIL for each; run it before a GPU run.
- `../work/tables/dev/` and `data-dev/` are the development exports of 10 tasks: 00576224 007bbfb7 0520fde7 1b59e163 2b9ef948 40f6cd08 543a7ed5 681b3aeb 7d18a6fb 90347967. A run on them needs `--indist 2`.
- The evidence tables `ex_input` and `ex_output` are built from the task JSON until the export gives them (request 20-8).

## Known faults in the method

- `--eval-limit` and `--train-limit` take the first tasks in file order, not a random sample. They are for code checks.
- In the exports up to 2812f3f5, `flows.parquet` numbers a relation's columns in another order than `tables.parquet` does (bug 20-134), and the loader joins the two by number. The field labels (computed, inferrable, remote, sole) and the column sources of every run that read the provenance (`pretrain-full-3` and `-4`, `value-s1` and `-s2`, `runs/remote-count/`) land on wrong columns in most cases, so their by-label and by-source numbers are not to be read until they are re-made on an export with the fix. The current files hold no column names that would let the loader remap.
- A streams export `data-full-<sha>` reads the converted solvers in `programs/arclog`, which a converter older than `<sha>` may have written: `data-full-9ccd4952` held a3f84088 in a form from before 4c962f62. A byte check of two streams exports therefore shows changes that no commit between their shas explains, until the stems are reconverted.
- In the exports up to 2a25e0c3, 1,180 of 6,720 cases (99 of 895 concept cases) do not reproduce their train outputs, with no fault line: the converter gives a wrong program (bugs 20-173, the Ray's node domain, and 20-174, a from-key input's clauses dropped), and no report gave the replay's verdict (the converter's train replay, `arclog_convert.train_replay`, reports it from change 20-180 on). Their streams are in the training data as gold.
- The exports made at a master before 76066d29, the merge of change 20-203, full-5529bd39 and data-full-5529bd39 among them, ran each request of every corpus replay under a 10 s wall-clock deadline. Under machine load a consistency test can run past that deadline and end `Stopped`: the case then has no grid columns and no fault line. Those of them made under load may hold such cases, the 5529bd39 pair among them; how many is not known without a rerun. Two full exports of one engine differed in 131 index rows this way.

## Tables for the encoder

- `arclog.corpus_tables` replays each case's stream and gives the tables of each decode as arrays. [export.py](../engine/arclog-py/tables_export.py) writes them as an index (Parquet) and int32 value files. Its docstring gives the layout. Each table's `token` column is its position in the case's stream, which is how a table joins its stream.
- The loader (`encoder.load_tables`) checks an export as it reads it:
    - Before it builds a cache, it reads the export's `checks.txt`. Each line of `checks.txt` reports a violation and an empty file reports none; where the file has a line, it refuses the build. Where the export has no `checks.txt`, it prints that and builds.
    - It compares its unseen tables and unseen entries by reason with the counts that the export's files give: tables with a fault, tables whose search for projections stopped, and entries of the kinds Undefined, Stopped, Fault and NoEntries. A difference fails the build.
    - It reads an entry's run of values only for a Rows entry, and the runs of its projections only for a Count entry. An entry of any other kind fails its case.
    - For each copy flow from a column of a table that the case holds, or from a DET, the column must have the source column's type. A copy flow whose `det` is not -1 reads a column of that DET of the table that holds the flow: its `source_column` indexes the table's `det_types[det]`. Where the export has an `orientation_types.json`, a column may also have an orientation type whose cells are a subset or a superset of the source type's, such as Dir from Dir8 or Dir8 from Dir. Where it has none, the types must be the same, and the printed counts say that the orientation subtypes were not known. Where every source of a column is such a copy flow, the column's value in each row must be a value of one of its sources, since its flows are its sources over every rule that derives it. A DET's values for a row are the rows of its relation at that row's own assignment of the DET. A source table with unseen entries leaves the values unchecked. The cache keeps the counts, and each load prints them. A violation fails the load, from a build or from the cache.
    - `--allow-check-violations` (train.py, pretrain.py and value_eval.py, off by default) reads an export through a violation of `checks.txt` or of the copy flows. The checks (`checks.sh`) read the dev export `work/tables/dev` strict; `value_check.py` reads its rollout exports, which predate the flows fixes, with the copy-flow check off.
- `../work/tables/2026-10-23-full/` holds the concept cases of 956 tasks: 21,165 relation tables. The 20-minute run wrote 10 GB of JSON. `../work/tables/2026-10-23/` holds a 10-task sample. Both directories have a superseded layout.
    - Tables per program: median 19, max 110.
    - Rows per table, summed over its examples and entries: median 48, p90 1,420, p99 13,453.
    - 22% of values are empty relations.
    - `Box` makes up 31% of the columns, `Color` 14%, `Number` 11% and `Unit` 10%.
- These tables precede 5f77b431, which gives the rows of a `DET`'s own relation at each of its values, so 754 of them carry a fault there. Rerun the corpus with export.py.
- An instance with no key clause gives its relations a `Unit` column that holds one value. The LM side drops that column when it reads the tables.
