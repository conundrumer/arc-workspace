# Evaluator and lib

Step 2 of the [build order](overview.md#build-order). It builds the evaluation of one case program at given parameter values, and the lib that evaluation runs over, as [lib-spec.md](../../docs/lib-spec.md) gives the lib. The parts are built in this order: joins, negation, `OR`, `IF`, `ALL`, the lib relations, the defaults of instance inputs, and then the components. A component comes after the components it uses, as Enclosure comes after Flood and Connectivity, and otherwise in the order of its frequency in the [survey](corpus-survey.md#instances). The components with no instance in `gen/` come last.

The step ends when the concept case of each solver, at the stored `param` records, gives on every pair the output grids that Souffle gives, as [verification.md](verification.md) checks it, or the difference is reported as a fault of the engine, of the converter, of the solver or of the Souffle lib, as the Principles requirement of [Requirements](overview.md#requirements) gives. This step reads the grid of a case with one model from `&output` by the grid columns of [The output set](../../docs/arclog/constraints.md#the-output-set). A concept case that holds a `ONE` or `CHOOSE` form has its models built in [One case](cases.md#one-case), and one that holds a `DEMONSTRATED` form in [Every case](cases.md#every-case), and that step's check covers it. A case program that the checker refuses for a cycle is reported as a fault of its solver, as [Recursion](corpus-survey.md#recursion) gives. The corpus run's time per solver is compared with Souffle's time in the snapshot, and each solver that runs slower is reported, as the speed requirement of [Requirements](overview.md#requirements) gives. This is the first check that the converter which wrote `gen/` keeps the outcomes of the Souffle solvers.

## Evaluation

### No recursion

- Each relation of the [dependency graph](reader.md#the-dependency-graph) is evaluated once from its inputs, in topological order. A checked case program is acyclic, so negation and `ALL` need no stratification beyond that order.
- Fixpoints exist only inside native constructions and in the rounds of `Unfold`.
- A `RIGID_DET` assembly gives its placements, as [Semantics](../../docs/arclog/rigid.md#semantics) gives.

### Joins

- The forms of a body compile to a clause IR, the evaluator's form of a body: conjunctive queries with negation and unions, over resolved relations. `IF` and `ALL` compile to the clauses below.
    - An `IF` compiles to two clauses, the else's with the test negated and its fresh labels projected away:
        ```
        out(P, C_in, Col) :- in(P, C_in), painted(P), fill_color(Col).
        out(P, C_in, Col) :- in(P, C_in), !painted(P), Col = C_in.
        ```
    - An `ALL` compiles to a counterexample relation and its negation, with the domain repeated as the existence test:
        ```
        rect_cex(B) :- span_box(B), in(P, Col), inside(P, B), !hole(Col).
        rect(B)     :- span_box(B), in(P, _), inside(P, B), !rect_cex(B), ...
        ```
        A body of several atoms compiles through one more relation that holds where the body holds.
- A clause is evaluated by generic join, which binds one variable at a time by intersecting the atoms that hold it. Its variable order is chosen per clause from the body's canonical form, as [Programs as values](reader.md#programs-as-values) gives, and the sizes of the relations it reads, so a body of equal content over relations of equal ids has one plan: the order of least estimated bindings, where a variable that a relation binds is estimated from the smallest distinct column count and the row counts divided by the domains of already placed variables, a variable that a union exports and no relation binds is estimated at the sum over the union's branches of the size of the smallest relation that binds it in the branch and stands only once its union grounds (each column of its branches that it does not export is placed, and each branch, with its placed columns bound, binds every variable it holds by an atom, by an algebra output of a satisfied mode, by an `=` from a bound variable, or by a union of the branch that grounds in turn), a variable that an algebra output or an `=` binds from placed variables costs nothing, and ties go to the variables in order of that size and then in canonical order. Each output of an algebra relation comes directly after the inputs of the first of its [modes](../../docs/lib-spec.md#algebra) that the order satisfies. Past 12 variables that a relation or a union binds, the order chooses the next by its estimate at the placed variables, and the export of a union stands once its union grounds. Where no export of a union meets that rule, as where a branch of one union reads a variable that only the exports of another ground, the order places the first export of a union that grounds with the branches' nodes left out that hold a column neither placed nor grounded by the branch. A join of a branch at bound columns that leave a column unbound leaves out the same nodes and gives the export's values of the rest, which the union's own check, at the rank of the last of its columns, holds to the whole branch. [Join orders](corpus-survey.md#join-orders) measures the order of the smallest relation, which the evaluator used before this one, and the computed-key index on the corpus. The order is provisional for the bodies that it does not measure.
- The join takes the nodes of a clause, and of each `NOT`, `ALL`, `IF` and `OR` branch inside it, in the canonical order of the conjuncts they compile, as [Programs as values](reader.md#programs-as-values) gives that order, and searches the branches of an `OR` in the canonical order of their paths. The bindings it counts, the rows it finds and the place where it stops at a cap then follow the clause's content, so two bodies that differ only in the order of their conjuncts count the same work. The IR of a clause body is compiled with its nodes in that order. Any other clause makes its copy in that order on its first join and keeps it. The relation lists its parts, and the rows of an `OR` that is a clause's whole body, in the order the clause is written in. A body that names a relation no form declares is `undefined`, whatever else it reads. The canonical order writes each relation name as written, so two clauses that read relations under other names can still be joined in different orders. A conjunct that a body repeats is joined as often as it is written.
- An algebra relation is evaluated at the point in the order where one of its modes is satisfied, as a native function, and its output is a variable like any other: a later atom joins on it through an index, and no clause recomputes it. Where two algebra outputs join on a computed key, as in the bodies that [Join shapes](corpus-survey.md#join-shapes) gives, the evaluator builds no index on the key: it checks the algebra atom at each binding of the other side. Souffle evaluates no algebra relation as a native function at a point in a join order, and two clauses of the corpus spend nearly all their time on that join, as [Causes of the outliers](outliers.md#causes-of-the-outliers) gives.
- Algebra relations declare which columns bound inputs can narrow and the margin ranges that hold at those inputs. The planner estimates those access paths and the join intersects their range indexes with the atoms' candidate runs. Alternatives within one range group are unioned. Planning builds distinct counts only for columns the atoms read.
- A negated atom, a counterexample relation of an `ALL` and an `IF` test are filters, probed once their outer labels are bound and with those bindings only. A test that reads an algebra relation through outer labels is not a finite relation alone, so none of them is evaluated alone.
- Inside a `NOT`, an `ALL`, an `IF` test or an `OR` branch, a label that the scope does not export is local. Once every variable left in the join order is local, the first binding that passes ends the search over them. The search visits values in their order as terms, which is their content order for every type but Ident. A search over an Ident label counts toward the binding cap every binding it would try, so the work counted does not depend on the order of interning. Souffle enumerates every one, as 20a9e565's `mirror_defect` does. A label at the top level of a body is a column of its relation, as [Clause](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#clause) gives, so every binding of it is kept.
- A safe subset of a body's atoms is a body, and the evaluator takes it as it is, whatever order the join visits its variables in. A prefix of a body that passes the checker is safe, since the safety condition follows the written order, as [Checker](reader.md#checker) gives. A subset that is not safe is refused, with the labels it leaves unbound. A label that the type check gives no type, such as `X` of `(= *X 0)` in a prefix of an `INIT` form whose head site for `X` is not yet written, is a variable of type `Int`, so a safe subset compiles whatever types its labels have. So a deletion report gives an atom whose deletion leaves a label unbound as needed for safety.
- A body's relation is given as its parts, one per connected set of its variables, as [Relations](reader.md#relations) holds it.
- Each part of a body, as [Factorized bodies](synthesis.md#factorized-bodies) gives it, is evaluated under two caps: a binding cap on the content it counts, the rows of the relations it reads and of the table it writes, and a row cap on the rows it holds. The content depends on the part's content alone, and not on the written order of its conjuncts or the order that its join searches in. The bindings that the join tries are effort, which only the request's effort guard bounds, as [Decode settings](synthesis.md#decode-settings) gives. A native construction counts the rows of its inputs and of its outputs, and checks the row cap over the rows it builds. A read that walks or indexes the product of a relation's parts counts the product's rows against the binding cap, as [Factorized bodies](synthesis.md#factorized-bodies) gives. Each outlier of the corpus's runs is a fault of its solver, of the lib or of Souffle's evaluation, and none is a cost of its intended concept, as [Causes of the outliers](outliers.md#causes-of-the-outliers) gives. Past the fixes, the largest relation is the orbit's cell pairs, as that section gives, and the slowest call alone is one that a fix shortened, as [Runs](corpus-survey.md#runs) gives. Souffle's time gives no count of the engine's work, and a row count gives no bytes, so both caps are fixed counts, set in this step from the largest counts of one evaluation among the solvers of the corpus that give counts, the extremes of [Runs](corpus-survey.md#runs) among them and the outliers left out, and neither depends on the machine. They are the defaults of the row cap and the binding cap of [Decode settings](synthesis.md#decode-settings). A margin outside a byte, as [Terms](reader.md#terms) gives, also stops an evaluation. The session's byte bound guards the machine: a stop at it stores no entry. The bound counts the larger of the session's ledger, which is the bytes of its intern table, its result cache and its reserved buffers, and, where the Python extension's allocator counts the heap, the ledger at the session's first check in the operation plus the heap's growth since then. The second count covers the rows and indexes that a table, a driver or a memo holds after the cache evicts them, and the scratch of a walk. A candidate program in synthesis runs under the same caps, and can be cancelled. When the evaluation reaches a bound, it stops with an error that names the clause and the bound, and the error is an alert, as [Bounds](overview.md#requirements) gives. A bound is not part of the [hypothesis](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#models-and-tasks), so the run gives no output for the case on that input. The judge reports the stop as a fault, and never compares it as `undefined`. In a domain walk, an evaluation stop rejects only that path. Assignments, table-work, session-byte, deadline and cancellation stops end the walk. Earlier rows stay available and the caller receives the first stop.

### The result cache

- The result cache is a memo table of evaluated relations, keyed by content. The map finds a key by its hash, and compares two keys whose hashes match by their content. The key of an entry holds:
    - the clause IR after name resolution, in canonical form,
    - for the entry of a clause's body or of a part of one, the type of each column of its relation, in the order of the cells of its rows, so that a hit gives cells under the types that the reader reads them with,
    - the id of each relation it reads, fact relations such as `input`, `ex_input` and `ex_output` among them, so that cases whose relations hold the same rows on an input share every entry that reads them, as [One program per case](performance.md#one-program-per-case) gives,
    - the parameter values it reads.
- A relation's id is interned by its rows: the hash combines the hashes of the rows in no order, and two relations whose hashes match are compared by their rows. So two relations share an id only when they hold the same rows.
- The value of an entry is the relation, or `undefined` where the clause reads a relation that no block of the case derives, as [Alts](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#alts) gives, or a stop at the binding cap, the row cap, a margin outside a byte or a variable that no node gives values. The caps are fixed for a session, so a stored stop holds for every later request of that entry. Each entry uses the same join plan from its own reads, whatever other bodies are cached. An extension of a stopped prefix is evaluated from its own reads. A query is one evaluation of a clause, finished or in progress, on one example at one assignment; its parts each read the evaluation caps, and there is no total over the queries of a program. A stop at the byte bound, at the table work cap or by a cancellation stores no entry, and no entry holds a partial relation. A decode holds a stop at the table work cap for bounded observations, as [Decode settings](synthesis.md#decode-settings) gives.
- A task session holds the cache as a concurrent map, bounded by a share of the session's byte bound. The share is provisional. A relation's bytes count once, however many entries read it, and they include the indexes built on it, summed at each eviction since an index is built after its relation is held. An eviction first drops the indexes of the relations whose indexes are largest, since an index is built again from its rows with no evaluation, and then drops entries by least recent use. The policy is provisional. An evicted relation is computed again when an entry asks for it, so eviction costs time and no correctness.
- The shared and incremental work of [Requirements](overview.md#requirements) comes from this cache:
    - The cases of a solver share each entry that no alt rederives.
    - The candidate programs of a decode share entries for their common forms and body parts.
    - A witness's `assign` replaces a parameter's entry, and the entries that read it are computed again.
    - A deletion report removes an atom, and the entries that read the clause are computed again.
    - The models of a `ONE` or `CHOOSE` differ only at the chosen relation's entry.
- Evaluation is on demand, from the entries asked for: `&output` on each example and the test against the pairs, or a relation that a command names. A decode asks for the rows of the body so far, as [Decode interface](synthesis.md#decode-interface) gives. A relation that no request reaches is not evaluated and reaches no bound, so a refine pass that needs every alert asks for every relation.
- Entries for parts of a body are built in [Synthesis](synthesis.md), as [Factorized bodies](synthesis.md#factorized-bodies) gives.

### Per-example relations

- A `RULE` relation is evaluated per example, and its entry reads that example's `input`. Its lifted form, as [Program](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#program) gives, is an entry that unions the per-example entries, with the Example column first. Where a per-example entry on an example the read reads is `undefined` or stopped, the lifted form is too. A read reads all the examples, or the train examples alone at a read that [Program](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#program) gives.
- A `DET` relation reads what [Program](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#program) lets it read, and its entry's key holds the ids of `ex_input` and `ex_output` where it reads them.
- The checker decides for each relation whether it has an Example column, from its reads and its key.

### Parallelism and determinism

- Independent entries evaluate in parallel on a work-stealing thread pool: examples, cases, candidate programs and models. A thread that asks for an entry another thread is computing blocks until that computation ends, and runs no other task while it waits, since a task run on the waiting thread's own stack can ask for an entry that a computation lower on that stack holds in flight. The wait follows only the reads of the entries, which form no cycle, so the pool cannot deadlock. Where that computation is cancelled, a waiting thread computes the entry itself.
- An Ident's number depends on the order of interning, which the thread count changes, and a writer numbers Idents by their contents, as [Terms](reader.md#terms) gives. Only equality on an Ident reaches an output, since the checker refuses an order on it, as [Checker](reader.md#checker) gives. A lib name's number depends on that order too. A cell that an output holds for an Ident or a lib name is the first 32 bits of the digest of its content or text (`TermId::export_encoding`), and a test of equality takes `TermId::key`. The `clippy.toml` of the crates that write outputs refuses `TermId::encoding`.
- No relation that an output reads depends on an order of term ids, of cells, of examples or of the rows that threads give, as [Invariance](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#invariance) and [Fidelity](../../docs/handbook/encoding.md#fidelity) require. A native construction gives a set, and none of its outputs reads the order it built the set in.
- Every hash map and hash set of the engine is an `arclog::hash::StableHashMap` or `StableHashSet`, whose hasher has a fixed seed. The `clippy.toml` of each engine crate refuses the standard map, the standard set and `RandomState`. The iteration order of a map still follows its keys, their insertion order and the layout of the table, so no output reads it.
- No code of a request reads the clock. `arclog::clock` holds the calls of `Instant::now`, `Instant::elapsed` and `SystemTime::now`, and the `clippy.toml` of each engine library crate refuses them elsewhere. A timing in a report is an `Elapsed` of a `Stopwatch`, and each use of the clock outside that module carries an `#[allow]` that gives its reason.
- Where the lib leaves a choice free, either each choice is a model, or no output observes the choice, or the output holds every choice. Quotient exports no representative, as [Quotient's `representative`](equivariance.md#quotients-representative) gives. The frame of a `RIGID_DET` is unobservable, since the checker types each pose, as [The `RIGID_DET` frame](equivariance.md#the-rigid_det-frame) gives. Wallpaper exports every shift of a coset of the period translations, so it chooses none, as [Wallpaper's shift](equivariance.md#wallpapers-shift) gives. Order gives tied members one rank and every tied member as minimal or maximal, and Distance and CellDistance give every predecessor on a least-cost path.
- So the same program on the same task gives the same rows and the same stops at the caps, at any thread count, and the rows move with the task under [Γ](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#invariance). A stop by a cancellation depends on the caller, and stores no entry.
- An Ident in an arcdata row comes from a Souffle run. Such rows are compared up to one renaming of Idents, a bijection over every Ident row of the solver, since one entity has one id, as [Determination](../../docs/handbook/solver-file.md#determination) gives.

## Lib

- Every algebra relation, table, construction and detector of [lib-spec.md](../../docs/lib-spec.md) is native Rust behind a trait. None of them is written in arclog, for two reasons: the language has no recursion to write a closure in, and native code runs faster. The proposers are built in [Determination](determination.md#proposers).
- The tables of the orientation algebra, the fact sets of [Algebra](../../docs/lib-spec.md#algebra), are fact relations embedded when the engine is compiled, as the lib registry is. The engine checks at startup that each table is D4-invariant.
- A construction is evaluated over its inputs grouped by key. Each one uses a conventional method, and its row in lib-spec.md gives what it computes:
    - Connectivity, Quotient, Enclosure and Flood: union-find and flood fill.
    - Propagation: forward chaining to a fixpoint.
    - Distance and CellDistance: breadth-first and Dijkstra search, keeping every predecessor at the least cost.
    - Order: a count of each member's predecessors under `before`, or a sort by score in which tied members share a rank.
    - BoundingBox: a minimum over margins.
    - Ray and Walk: stepping.
    - Boundary: a test of each cell's neighbors.
    - Outlines: candidate boxes from corner cells, each tested along its perimeter.
    - Agreement: a comparison under each correspondence. Periods, Occurrences and Wallpaper use Agreement.
    - Scales: a test of each candidate scale against the picture.
    - Division, Tiling, Frame and ExtentDivisions: arithmetic.
    - UnitPropagation and PathInverseConsistency: fixpoints over candidate sets, by the propagation rules of [The constraint module](cases.md#the-constraint-module).
    - Cardinality and Excess: counts. Singleton: a test that the members agree. Inclusion: counts over the pairs that share a member.
- `Unfold` iterates over states. A state is the cursor `t` with its `taken`, `untaken` and `excluded` sets. At each round, the clause evaluator evaluates the `step`, `skip` and `take` bodies against the current states, and a `NOT` in those bodies reads the current state. A `step` moves the cursor and one item, and a `skip` moves the cursor alone, as the row of `Unfold` gives. A state already reached is not expanded again, and the states are finite, so the iteration ends.
- Where a `score` ties, the walk forks. Each walk, a path of states from the start, is one tiebreak resolution of the case's output, as [Alt outputs](../../docs/handbook/solver-file.md#alt-outputs) gives, and its outputs are the facts along its path. The bodies read only a state's content and the key's inputs, so walks that reach states of equal content share that state's computation. The instance's entry holds the states and their edges. The distinct outputs of the walks are collected over that graph, each state's once, and the collection stops where [Enumeration and its stops](cases.md#enumeration-and-its-stops) gives, as [Models](cases.md#models) gives a joint walk. Souffle's `Take` takes every tied item in one step instead, which departs from Principles. The fork's cost has no evidence, since no input of the corpus ties: 3490cc26 holds the one instance with a `score`, and in its runs each state has one item of least score.
- A frame of cells, or a frame cut into equal tiles, is an instance of a construction of the lib, such as Frame, Tiling or Division, whose inputs read the `DET`s that the program states for its fields, as [Program](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#program) gives. No field is a stored value.
- Each lib element is compared with the Souffle lib by the corpus run and by [Lib comparison](verification.md#lib-comparison). Where the two differ, the engine is wrong, the Souffle form is an [engine device](../../docs/lib-spec.md#engine-devices), or the Souffle form departs from Principles. The arcdata record and Principles decide which. Where the two agree, that does not show that either is equivariant, as [Equivalence and subsumption](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#equivalence-and-subsumption) gives for agreement on some inputs. UnitPropagation, PathInverseConsistency and Scales have no instance in `gen/` and no Souffle form, so a test against their rows in lib-spec.md checks them.

## How each decision can fail

| Decision | Failure | What catches or bounds it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The lib is native | a construction's semantics drift from the Souffle lib | the corpus run and the lib comparison, from the first construction on |
| Generic join with a static variable order | a clause over a pair relation or a cross product blows up | the binding cap and the row cap. The native Order builds no pairs, for its score form, for `first` and for a lexicographic order that no clause reads by `before`, where Souffle's `before` form built 2.44 million pairs in a57f2f04. Among the 8 solvers of the survey's runs whose calls take under 0.2 s, the largest relation is 269e22fb's `occ.placement`, 31414 rows of candidate placements |
| The result cache holds every relation | memory grows over a long session | eviction by bytes; a key is its content, so an evicted entry costs a new computation and no correctness |
| Determinism at any thread count | an Ident, an order or a stop depends on the thread schedule | Idents compared only by equality and written by content; caps that count work and rows, not time; each free choice of the lib removed, a model or given whole, as [Free choices](equivariance.md#free-choices) gives; a command runs the corpus at one thread and at many, and compares |
