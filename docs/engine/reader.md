# Reader and checker

Step 1 of the [build order](overview.md#build-order). It builds the reader, the resolver, the writer, the representations that the later steps evaluate, and the checks that evaluation needs. The present Python lint keeps serving the corpus, and its other checks move to the engine in [Synthesis](synthesis.md) and [Tooling](tooling.md), as [Checker](#checker) gives.

The step begins with `gen/` converted again from the solvers on `master`, so that no file reads a lib name the registry lacks.

The step ends when the engine and the Python lint agree on `gen/` for the checks this step builds: each error the lint reports from one of those checks, the engine reports at the same place, and the engine reports no other. Where the two differ, the difference is a fault of the engine or of the lint, as [Principles](../../docs/handbook/principles.md) decide. Every file the lint passes reads, resolves and round-trips. A check that the lint lacks, such as the comparison typing rule and the pose rule, reports nothing on `gen/` apart from the faults of the survey, such as the cycles of [Recursion](corpus-survey.md#recursion), and a test feeds it a file that breaks it and asserts the refusal.

## Solvers

- The reader reads a solver's `.arclog` and `.arcdata` files into one `Solver` tree: the claims, the tree of alts, the arcdata records, and `cases()`.
- `cases()` gives each case program as a program value, on demand, so a run builds only the cases it runs. It gives the cases that the arcdata file's `exclude` forms leave, and an excluded case on request, as [Cases](cases.md#cases) gives. To make a case program, it removes the claims, puts the forms that the alt rederives in the place of the concept program's forms, and adds the forms of each claim the alt adds, with the exceptions that [The `ALTS` form](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#the-alts-form) gives:
    - A `DET` of the concept program reads the concept program's derivation in every case, so it is evaluated once, in the concept program, and every case reads its parameter as one node of the [dependency graph](#the-dependency-graph), never its body, with the values that [`DET`](determination.md#det) keeps for it.
    - A `DET` that an alt rederives replaces the concept program's `DET` of that relation, as a `RULE` does, and the case reads a relation as the forms of the blocks of greatest depth derive it.
    - A combination of alts takes the forms of its nested `ALT`.
- The resolver then resolves the names of each case program. A relation that no block of the case derives resolves to an undefined relation, as [The `ALTS` form](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#the-alts-form) gives, and the case is undefined on each input where a clause reads it.
- The forms of a solver with a `representative` line, as the arcdata [Header](../../docs/arclog/arcdata.md#header) gives, are read under the representative's claims. Where the line gives a case's name, `cases()` gives that case alone.
- A `DET_OR_RULE` or `RULE_OR_DET` line is read as an `ALTS` form on its relation, as [Stored readings](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#stored-readings) gives, which splits only the bases that read the relation, as [Cases](cases.md#cases) gives.
- The writer writes a `Solver` tree as the two files. It writes a synthesized program as an arclog file in the program form and the s-expression layout, with names in place of token ids: a label takes the name of the column or field of its first site, PascalCase, with a digit suffix where the clause already has that name, and a relation slot is written by its index, since no token of a decode states what the relation means. The tree keeps the comments and the strings, and the writer lays out each form as the converter does. So reading a file of `gen/` and writing it again gives the same bytes. The formatting of a hand-written file into that layout is built in [Tooling](tooling.md#formatting).

## Representations

### Terms

- A task session is the engine's state for one task: its [result cache](evaluator.md#the-result-cache) and its intern table.
- Every column value is a 32-bit term id:
    - A number is its two's-complement value.
    - A Color, Example or orientation value is its constant. A pose is the constant of its D4 element, which the pose rule of [Checker](#checker) keeps from every read. The Unit value is one constant.
    - A Box is its four margins, as four signed bytes in one term id. A margin outside a byte is a reached bound, as [Bounds](overview.md#requirements) gives: no plausible program reaches one on a grid of ARC's size, and the margins in the relation rows of the corpus's runs lie well inside a byte, as [Box values](corpus-survey.md#box-values) gives. An algebra relation computes in 32-bit integers and packs only the Boxes it binds, so a margin outside a byte that it passes through on the way is no bound.
    - An Ident is the index of the content it labels in the task session's intern table, keyed by the whole content with the type of its members, so two contents never share an id, and neither do the colors `{1, 2}` and the numbers `{1, 2}`. The table finds a content by its hash, and compares contents only where two hashes match. An Ident read from a file, such as one of an arcdata record, is interned by its type and its written value, so it equals only the Idents read as that value of that type. The number depends on the order of interning, and only equality on it reaches an output. The intern table also keeps each content's member digest: a hash of the member type's column types and of the members, where a member's hash holds the digest of each Ident in it. Equal contents have one digest in every session, and a table export gives its first 32 bits as the Ident's cell.
    - A writer, such as the arcdata writer or the trace form's writer, numbers the Idents it writes by the order of their contents' encodings, from the inside out: an Ident whose content holds other Idents is numbered after them, and its content is ordered by their numbers. So the text is the same from run to run. A value read from a file, such as an Ident of an arcdata record, is written as it was read, and the writer numbers the other Idents of that type in the file after the largest such value, so each number read back stands for one content.
- The engine has no record values. A key or a type parameter bound to several fields takes one column per field, as a site names them by projection, `(:a (:E *Ep_e) (:Tile *Ep_tile))`. A component works over a short array of term ids at each such column group. An arcdata value written as a list is read into the same columns.
- A row is a fixed-arity array of term ids. Types are static: the checker holds them, and a row has no type tag.

### Relations

- A relation is a set of rows of one arity. A body's relation is held as its parts, as [Factorized bodies](synthesis.md#factorized-bodies) gives, and each part is stored row after row in one array with a hash table of row numbers over it. An index is a trie over the columns in one order, held as sorted arrays with one level per column, built on first use in the order that a clause's variable order asks for, and kept with the relation.

### Programs as values

- A program, a clause, a body and an atom are immutable values. Two values are equal when their resolved content is equal, whatever file or decode they came from. Names and order do not enter the content: a body's content is its canonical form under a reorder of its atoms, a reorder of the branches of an `OR`, and a renaming of its labels, which individualization–refinement gives: color refinement with the column paths as edge labels, then the first largest cell that is not a singleton, with a cap of 1000 search nodes. The refinement and the selection rest on the corpus, where the search stays far under the cap, as [Body symmetry](corpus-survey.md#body-symmetry) gives. The cap is provisional: it guards synthesized bodies, whose symmetry the corpus does not show. A form whose search passes the cap has no canonical labeling that is free of its written order, so it is refused with a labeling bound that names the cap, and it gets no hash that a caller reads: the check refuses it, a decode refuses the token that completes it, and `query_hash` raises `arclog.LabelingBoundReached`. The gauge `labeling_nodes` ([Counters](synthesis.md#decode-settings)) measures the most search nodes that a search reaches. A derived relation's content is its clause, with its columns in the canonical positions of their labels, and the resolver turns each column name that a read gives into its position. A symmetry of a clause gives it several labelings of least form, and so several orders of its columns under which its content is the same. The rows take one of those orders, and a read of a relation of one clause reads the same rows in each of them. So the content of a read writes each atom on such a relation in the order of its columns that gives the atom the least bytes, and the graph of the reader labels the edge of each site of that atom by the least of its column's positions in those orders. A reader's content then does not depend on the written order of the clause it reads, and it keeps each column's position, so two different reads do not get one content. Each syntax node holds a hash of its canonical form, and two nodes whose hashes match are compared by canonical form. Every hash in the engine uses one fixed function with no random seed, so hashes are the same from run to run.
- A node's places in the files, the names of its labels, and its order of atoms and branches are kept beside it, outside its hash, so the writer gives back the file's bytes. The exception is the name of each column that a label gives a declared relation: a later site reads the column by that name, so the node's hash holds it as written, and the resolved content holds the column's position in its place. An edit names the node it changes by its path: its form, and its index in the source order of each list above it. An error at a node that stands in several places is reported at each of them.
- An edit adds, removes or replaces an atom or a site. It gives a new value and leaves the old one.
- A partial atom, a relation with the sites given so far, is a well-formed atom whose other columns are projected away, and a partial atom of an algebra relation is one only once it has a site at each column of one of its modes. A site added to a positive atom gives a subset of its rows. So the completion's rows, projected onto the prefix's labels, lie among the rows of the prefix's positive atoms at the top level of its body, each as soon as it stands. An `OR`, an `IF`, an `ALL` and a `NOT` bound the completion only once complete, since a branch, a test atom or a site added to them can give more rows.
- The reader, the decoder and any search procedure build programs through these values and edits. Evaluation takes a program value, not a file.

### The dependency graph

- After name resolution, a case program is a directed graph. A clause reads only relations declared before it, so in a single program the order of declaration is a topological order, and a cycle can stand only in a case program built from alts. Its nodes are the fact relations, clause relations, instance inputs and outputs, parameters, chosen relations, `RIGID_DET` assemblies and `DEMONSTRATED` relations, and each read is an edge. The checker refuses a cycle, so a checked case program is acyclic.
- An `Unfold` instance, with its `step`, `skip` and `take` inputs, which read its outputs, is one node.

## Checker

- The checker is a set of checks. Each check reads one syntax node and its scope: the declarations, labels and types visible at that node. The cycle check reads the dependency graph instead. Each check has a form on a prefix, which refuses a prefix only where no completion passes the check, as a viable prefix of a parser is. So the token mask of [synthesis.md](synthesis.md#token-mask) runs the same checks on a partial program. This step gives each check an interface that admits its prefix form, and [Synthesis](synthesis.md) builds the prefix forms.
- A form's check result is cached on the form's canonical hash and the signatures of the names it reads, so the cases that share a form share its check, whatever alts come before it: a form that every case of af24b4cc shares is checked once. The safety condition reads the written order, which the canonical form leaves out, so its result is cached on the form's hash with its atom order.
- This step builds the checks that evaluation needs: the grammar, the scope, the columns of each site, the types, the pose rule of [The `RIGID_DET` frame](equivariance.md#the-rigid_det-frame), the labels that `=` makes and that an `OR` or an `IF` exports, the safety condition, the rule of an `Unfold` instance's reads of its outputs, as [Construction](../../docs/lib-spec.md#construction) gives, cycles, an instance's input order and defaults, and the pooled and lifted reads of [Program](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#program).
- The type check infers each label's type over the lattice of the types: `C4` under `D4`, `Dir` and `DiagDir` under `Dir8`, `Axis` and `DiagAxis` under `Axis4`, and `Position` and `Offset` under `Box`. A label's type is the meet of the types of its sites other than those of `NOT` atoms, and, where no other site gives it a type, the meet of the types of its `NOT` atoms' sites. A `NOT` site whose type has an empty meet with the label's type is refused. A label that an `OR` or an `IF` exports takes the join over its branches. `0` takes the type of the label or the column it stands at. An instance makes its component's type parameters concrete by its binding, and each `RIGID_DET` assembly has its own Pose type. The meet is taken per branch, with the label's sites outside the form, so a branch whose meet is empty is refused as well as a label whose meet is empty: neither can hold a value. A label that only `(= *X 0)` gives a type takes the type of the instance input it stands at, or of the column that another clause of the relation gives it, and the check cache's key then holds that clause's signature. A Pose type is below and above no other type, and `d4_elem`'s column takes the type of its label, as the pose rule gives.
- The safety condition is checked in one pass over each body in its written order, with the modes of the algebra relations, over the `=` classes, with `0` bound: a site of a relation over an infinite domain binds its label only where a mode of the relation has its other labels bound by the atoms written before it, and a `NOT` and an `ALL` stand after the atoms that bind their outer labels. So each prefix of a body that passes is safe too. An algebra atom may leave out an output, and leaves out an input only where a mode of the relation does. A label that an `OR` or an `IF` exports is bound outside it only where every branch binds it, the then and the else for an `IF`, and an `ALL` and a `NOT` bind nothing outside them.
- The comparison typing rule of the type check refuses `<`, `<=`, `>` and `>=` on every type but `Int` and `Nat`, the types of numbers, as `admits_order` of the type lattice gives. A color has identity and no other properties, as [Color](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#color) gives. An order on an orientation value, an Ident, a Box or an Example would be an order of the engine's encoding or of the task file, which [Fidelity](../../docs/handbook/encoding.md#fidelity) and [Invariance](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#invariance) rule out.
- The other checks of the language, such as where `(= *X 0)` may stand and the forms of `ONE`, `CHOOSE`, `RIGID_DET` and `DEMONSTRATED`, move from the Python lint in [Synthesis](synthesis.md), since the token mask runs every check of the language, except the checks of `DEMONSTRATED`, which no token stream holds, and which move in [Tooling](tooling.md). The [second spellings](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#second-spellings) are suggestions and not rules of the language, so the lint never refuses them, and only the [token mask](synthesis.md#token-mask) can block them.
- The checker refuses a cycle in the dependency graph of a case program, and the token mask needs no cycle test, since a decode writes one program, whose clauses read only relations declared before them. A cycle includes a `DET` that reads a relation which depends on its own parameter, since such a `DET` predicts through the program at guessed values.
- The engine's lint reads each file once, resolves each case program, and then runs every check. It reports each error once per place in the file, with the cases it holds in.
- The lib registry supplies the names of the lib. It is embedded from a tracked file under `engine/lib/` when the engine is compiled, so the wheel reads no file for it, and a test parses it, so a fault of the file fails the engine's tests. That file is written from [lib-spec.md](../../docs/lib-spec.md) and declares its lib, with the Pose type and `pose_difference`, and no `representative`. An algebra relation, a relation of the box algebra or the orientation algebra of [Algebra](../../docs/lib-spec.md#algebra), the group relations included, declares its columns, types and modes. A construction or a detector declares its type parameters, its inputs in the order the component declares them, with their defaults, and its outputs. A native proposer declares its columns, and a proposer written in arclog, as [Proposers](determination.md#proposers) gives, has its clause's labels as its columns, in the order they first stand in the clause. A `DET_OR_RULE` line maps a proposer's columns by that order onto its first proposer's columns, as [Stored readings](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#stored-readings) gives, so a case that reads the line by another proposer reads the relation at that proposer's columns. A new lib element adds a registry entry and changes no checker code.
- The converter writes each solver under `gen/`. The engine evaluates each lib name natively, a name that [lib-spec.md](../../docs/lib-spec.md#conventions) defines from other lib names included.
- The checks over a solver's structure, such as the claims, the nesting of alts and the arcdata names, are built in [Tooling](tooling.md), as [Checks as queries](tooling.md#checks-as-queries) gives.
- The Python bindings raise `arclog.Refusal` where the engine refuses its input, and `arclog.Refusal` is no `ValueError`. `StudyProgram.from_sexp`, `StudyProgram.from_stream` and `rigid_slot_name` raise `arclog.CheckRefused`, a subclass of `arclog.Refusal`, for a program that the reader, the resolver or a check refuses. The other calls on a `StudyProgram`, a mutation or a `StudyTaskEvaluator` that refuse their input, such as `construct_identity`, `draw_specs` and `StudyTaskEvaluator.from_stream`, raise `arclog.Refusal`. A failure inside the engine, such as a panic in a table evaluation or a call on a program that the engine has checked, raises `arclog.Fault`. A panic in a call on a session ends that session: the panic leaves the session's caches as they were at the panic, so each later call on the session, on its decoders and their forks, and on the tables of those, raises `arclog.SessionDead` at once and reads none of them. Only a new session serves calls again. A request that passes the effort guard raises `arclog.EffortExhausted`, and a call that evaluates several candidates and passes a bound over the whole call raises `arclog.CallGuardExhausted` ([Synthesis](synthesis.md)); these, `arclog.SessionDead` and `arclog.Fault` are subclasses of `arclog.EngineFault`. A `CheckRefused` has the attribute `check`, the name of the first check that refused the program, such as `Picks`, and the attribute `at`, the path of the node as a list of indexes, or `None` where the refusal stands at a place in the text; then `line` and `column` give that place. A `ValueError` from the engine is a wrong argument of the caller, and a caller does not catch it.

## How each decision can fail

| Decision | Failure | What catches or bounds it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The intern table lives as long as the task session | memory grows over a long synthesis session | the table's bytes count toward the session's bound, an entry is freed once no cached relation, decode state or trace holds its id, a freed number is not used again in the session, and the bound reached is an alert |
| The canonical form by individualization–refinement | a body with much symmetry takes exponential time | in the corpus, refinement leaves a tie in few bodies, each a real symmetry, and the search stays far under the cap, as [Body symmetry](corpus-survey.md#body-symmetry) gives; the provisional cap of 1000 nodes guards synthesized bodies, and a form that passes the cap is refused |
| One set of checks serves the lint and the token mask | a missing rule admits a wrong program or token, or refuses a right one | a wrong refusal shows on `gen/`, which the lint must pass and the token mask must admit in full; a wrong admission shows when the evaluator gets a program it refuses, since every evaluator entry point checks its input |
