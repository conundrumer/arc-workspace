# arclog expansion

How the engine reads two forms of an [arclog](../arclog/arclog.md) file into the relations its streams declare.

## Zero columns

[Negation and constraints](../arclog/arclog.md#negation-and-constraints) gives where `(= *X 0)` and a site on `0` may stand. The decoder lowers a column of `0` as follows.

The decoder reads a case's program alone, and there a column that is `0` in every clause of its relation is dropped, with each `(= *X 0)` that made it. Each read of the relation leaves out its site on the column and reads `0` at each other site of that site's label, and a head column whose label is then `0` in every clause drops in turn. Where the dropped site's label is a `^` label of a key clause, the key drops that column, and each read of the keyed relation leaves out that field. A prediction drops a key column together with the catalog's key; its value columns and rule fields stay. The lowering leaves a `DEMONSTRATED` form and its reads as they are, and keeps their zero columns. A relation left with no column and no atom is true, and the reads of it leave out its atom. A column stays, with its `(= *X 0)`, where `0` may not stand at a site that reads its label: an input's head site, the first label of a constraint, a `ONE` mark, a site on a column that is not a number or a Box of a fact or a lib relation, a read under an `OR`, `NOT`, `IF` or `ALL` that does not force the label, a read in `CHOOSE` or `RIGID_DET`, or a clause of a port. A key column stays where the engine fills an input from the key and the instance writes no clause for it. The decoder blocks each top-level `(= *X 0)` of a `RULE` or a `DET`, so a case that keeps such a column gets no stream. It blocks `(= *X 0)` in a later branch, an else, a test and a list of an `ALL` form, as it blocks `(= *X Y)`.

## Stored readings

[Stored readings](../arclog/arclog.md#stored-readings) gives the `DET_OR_RULE` and `RULE_OR_DET` lines. The reading below is the expansion that the engine and the writer give a line.

The stored reading is a `DET` of the relation whose rows are the rows that the body's derivation gives at every train example, over the columns that a read of the relation names in any case, the Example column left out, as the column rule of a `DET` gives in [Sites](../arclog/arclog.md#sites): a `RULE` of the body under a fresh name `&<r>_derived` (the helper), and `(DET &r (ex_output (:E *E)) (&r_derived (:E E) ...) (ALL ((ex_output (:E *E2))) ((&r_derived (:E E2) ...))))`, with a site on each of those columns of the helper. A `DET` of the concept program that reads `&r` reads the helper, and so does each other read of `&r` with a site on `E`. Where a block of the case derives `&r` anew with a `RULE`, that `RULE` is the helper, with its head under the fresh name, and the `DET` stands in that block, in the place of the `RULE`. A name that another relation holds gets a numeral after the suffix. A line with no body that stands on no port keeps the one clause `(DET &r)`, which the writer reports as a fault of the conversion.

On an output, the instance stays, the `DET` is named `&<instance>_<port>_stored`, and each read of the port other than a `DET` of the concept program's, and other than a read with a site on `E`, reads that `DET`. Where the instance is task-wide in the case, as an alt's stored reading of a relation it reads can make it, the port has no Example column, and the `DET` is `(DET &<instance>_<port>_stored (&<instance>.<port> ...))`, the port's rows, with a site on each column the form above names. In dd6b8c4b, `&capacity` reads only `&box`, so at `&box=$Stored` the `&capacity.size=$Stored` `DET` is `(DET &capacity_size_stored (&capacity.size *N))`. On an input, the derivation is a helper over the input's clause, the helper is `&<instance>_<port>_derived`, the `DET` is `&<instance>_<port>_stored`, and the `INIT` form's clause of that input is replaced by a read of the `DET`, since the instance still declares the port, and the stream declares a relation once for each form that derives it.

The stored reading of a `RULE_OR_DET` line on an output of an instance that has a key clause has value columns: the key's fields, each a field of the port's `key` column, and each other column of the port that every read of the port in the case, other than a `DET` of the concept program's and other than a read with a site on `E`, names with the label that the same read gives a field of the key. A column that a read names with another label is a key column of the join. In a5f85a15, `&diagonal.ray` is an output of `&diagonal`, keyed by `^D`: `(&diagonal.ray (:key (:D L)) P (:D L) *K (:Origin *E))` names `D` with `L`, the label of the key's field `D`, and `(&diagonal.ray (:key *D) *P D *K)` names it with `D`, the field's elided label, so the value columns are `D` of `key` and `D`, and `P`, `K` and `Origin` are joined from the port on the input. That stored reading has two forms. The `DET` `&<instance>_<port>_value` determines the rows of the value columns alone, with the other columns existential, each field of a record column as a column of its own named by the record's name and the field's, `(:key D)` as `KeyD`, made fresh against the columns that a read names. The `RULE` `&<instance>_<port>_stored` joins those rows with the rows of the other columns that the port gives on the input. Each read of the port other than a `DET` of the concept program's, and other than a read with a site on `E`, reads that `RULE`, a site on a record column as a site on the flat column of each field it names. Where the port has no Example column in the case, the `DET` `&<instance>_<port>_value` is the port's rows at the value columns.
