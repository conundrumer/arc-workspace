# Synthesis

The step of the [build order](overview.md#build-order) after [Determination](determination.md). It builds token ids and the vocabulary, a writer from a case program to its token stream, the decoder and its token mask, the prefix forms of the checks and the checks of the language that [Checker](reader.md#checker) leaves to the Python lint, the traces, entries in the result cache for parts of a body, the Python package, the wheel, and a run on Kaggle. It rests on [Token stream](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#token-stream) and [Trace form](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#trace-form).

The step ends when each of these holds, or the difference is reported as a fault, as the Principles requirement of [Requirements](overview.md#requirements) gives:

- The decoder admits the token stream that the writer gives each case program of `gen/`, in full, or through the `END` of its first `OUT` form that holds, as [Decode interface](#decode-interface) gives, with the tokens after it no part of the program the decode reads. A case program that holds a fault of its solver or of the conversion that [Determination](determination.md) names is reported as that fault.
- At the `END` of each such stream's `OUT` form, the consistency result equals the solves outcome of the case at the values that [Determination](determination.md) determines.
- The rows of each prefix of those streams equal the rows that the evaluator gives the prefix's sub-body.
- The concept case program of aba27056, run in the Kaggle notebook on its task with the test output held out, gives one attempt on its test input, and that attempt is the test output.

## Decode settings

- The decode settings are one value, which a decode reads, and each setting has a default. The mask, the consistency test, the rule tests or the tables of [Decode interface](#decode-interface) read a setting, and no other part of the design depends on its value, so the synthesis work can change one with no change to the design. A setting is one that has a reason to take two values, as [Switches](#switches) gives.
    - **Vocabulary caps.** The relation, column and label slots of [Token mask](#token-mask). Default: the corpus's maxima with a margin. The largest counts over the 942 concept cases of `gen/` that give a stream are 201 relations declared (a64e4611), 23 column positions (a57f2f04) and 28 labels of one form (28a6681f). Each cap is the least power of two at or above twice its count: 512, 64 and 64. The margin is provisional: no decode shows how far a synthesized program goes past the corpus. The mask reads them.
    - **Assignment cap.** Default: the cap of [`DET`](determination.md#det). The consistency test reads it, at each prefix.
    - **Model cap and node cap.** Default: the caps of [The constraint module](cases.md#the-constraint-module). The consistency test reads them, at each prefix.
    - **Row cap and binding cap.** Default: the caps of [Joins](evaluator.md#joins). One query evaluates a clause, finished or in progress, on one example at one assignment. Its evaluations read these caps on every path, including verification, table acquisition, determination, the mask and attempts. The binding count of an evaluation is its content count: the rows of the relations that it reads and the rows of the table that it writes. It depends on the evaluation's content alone, so a query gets one verdict whatever its written order, the join's variable order, the other cached entries and the assignment order. The bindings that a join tries are not in it: they are effort, which the effort guard below bounds. The request work cap sums the content of a request's queries.
    - **Projection cap.** Default: 10,000 rows, provisional: no measurement yet gives the smallest cap at which each read of the gold solvers that lies outside every maximal projection is a pairing inside a search over candidates. The maximal projections of [Decode interface](#decode-interface) read it.
    - **Table work cap.** Default: 2^21 bindings, the writer's value, so a stop at the cap is the same in a check, a readback and the writer; provisional. This budgets observations: extents. Relation and body queries keep a work count with no total cap across examples or assignments. An extent derivation has its own count, under this setting times the number of table assignments, with an empty assignment domain counted as one. A stop of an extent derivation at that budget or an evaluation bound leaves the rows available and records the stop. The row comparisons of a study have their own bounded counts. Observation evaluations hold table-work stops by result-cache key for later bounded observations of the decode; row queries do not read those stops.
    - **Prefix content cap.** Default: 2^18 units of content (the engine's `PREFIX_CONTENT_CAP`; the setting `prefix_content_cap` of `DecodeSettings` moves it), provisional until the cap audit, and far below the request work cap. It caps the content that one prefix check counts: the admission of a written `DET`, the mask's walk at a `DET` close. The check counts the content units that the request work cap lists, and a hit charges the units its entry recorded. It counts inside the scope where no other count adds. A join that the check runs also stops as it produces rows, once the rows it holds, with the content counted so far, would pass the cap. Its stop is a [`ReachedBound::TableWork`](../../engine/arclog/src/eval/values.rs) that no cache stores, and a check that it stops admits its token, the decision falling to the form's end. The check depends on the content of the finished forms alone, so it is no part of any count that cuts a result. The gauge `prefix_content` gives the largest content a check counted.
    - **Rule tests.** Default: off. With it off, no `RULE` end runs a test of its relation. With it on, the `END` of each `RULE` clause gives, beside the step's other results, the verdict of the test of the clause's relation: the search for one Position column and one Color column of the relation, its grid columns, under which the grids, read model by model, solve every train pair, as [Solves](cases.md#solves) gives for the run's candidates, at each joint assignment of the `DET`s the relation reads. The verdicts are `Consistent`, `NotConsistent` where no pair of columns solves the pairs or the assignments keep none, `SeveralValues` where a `DET` without `(ONE)` takes more than one value over the consistent assignments, `Fault`, where two pairs of columns solve the pairs and give different output sets on an input of the task, which makes the grid columns underdetermined, or a run faults, and `Stopped` at a cap. The verdict ends no decode, makes no relation the output, and changes no mask, so the setting changes no token that a decode admits and no verdict at an `OUT` form. The cost of the search is that of [The grid columns at each clause's end](performance.md#the-grid-columns-at-each-clauses-end). The consistency test of the `OUT` form reads none of it. The decode reads the setting at a `RULE` clause's end.
    - **Request work cap.** Default: 2^30 units of work, provisional: the data of the gold programs' requests sets it, and [Switches](#switches) gives its margin once that data is in. A request is one call from Python or the CLI into a decoder or a study evaluator: `admissible`, `admits`, `forced`, `step`, `rows`, `counts`, `projections`, `attempts`, `step_tables`, `successor_tables`, `gold_tables` and the like. The request's work is the content count, summed over every query, walk and observation that the call runs, so the cap bounds a request that no cap of one query bounds. `arclog.work_total()` sums it over the requests of the process: a measure that the sampler reports, not a budget. The count is a plain sum of the charges, so it and the place where it passes the cap do not depend on the machine's speed. A hit in a cache, in a memo or in the result cache charges the cost recorded in its entry, which is what the entry's computation charged, so the count is the work of the call with every cache empty. The determinism of the count, below, gives this. A unit is one of these:
        - A row that a join reads, counted once for each node that reads it: the rows that the relations of the body's part hold, as they are stored, nested filters and unions among them. A native construction counts the rows of its inputs the same way.
        - A row that a join or a native construction writes: the rows that the parts of the table it gives hold. The product of a relation of several parts counts its rows where a read walks it.
        - A row that a walk of rows, a materialization, a union of the forms of a relation, the placing of a proposer's rows, the read of a grid or an extent derivation visits.
        - A complete assignment of a walk of joint assignments, with one for each `DET` it assigns, and the rows of the domain of an open `DET`. The steps that reach an assignment, and the square of the rows of that domain, which the walk compares in pairs, are effort.
        - The rows that an instance's input reads and writes; the rows that an `Unfold` reads for its keys and inputs and the states of its state graphs, with the states of each walk of a graph; the rows of the options of a chosen or `DEMONSTRATED` relation and of each model that an enumeration draws; the groups of the rows that the candidate values of an atom are read for, and the rows of the product where they read several parts; the rows of each part that a search over projections reads.
        - A copy of a buffer is held as [Byte charges](#decode-settings) gives, since a copy takes time in proportion to its bytes and the budgets limit them.
        - No other work is a unit. The bindings of a search, the rows that an index build walks, the steps of a search over projections or of a completion search, and the candidate tokens that the mask checks are effort. The prefix checks of the mask, of the static replacements and of the admission of written `DET`s run in a scope where the content they count adds to no cutting count and none to the effort, which has only the bindings their searches try, since their work depends on the forms that the decode has finished and not on the form a count is the count of. The admission of a written `DET` counts its content under the prefix content cap alone. An entry that such a check computes records the content it counted, and a hit on it in a counted request charges it.
    - **Effort guard.** Default: 2^34 bindings tried (the engine's `EFFORT_CAP`; the setting `effort_cap` of `DecodeSettings` moves it), provisional: far above the bindings that any gold program's request tries, and the gauge `effort` gives them. It counts the bindings that the searches of a request try, the rows that index builds walk and the steps and candidates of the searches above, and it stops a runaway. No result depends on it: a request that passes it stops every later charge (`ReachedBound::Effort`), no cache keeps what it cut short, and the call raises `arclog.EffortExhausted` in place of giving any result. `arclog.EffortExhausted` is a subclass of `arclog.EngineFault`, as is `arclog.Fault`; it carries `gauge`, the gauge's name (`effort`), and `cap`. A hit in a cache charges no effort, since it tries nothing, so the effort of a request depends on the caches and the content count does not. The candidates of one call share the call's effort. Every call from Python that runs a request raises it, whatever thread the request ran on: the replays of `stream_tables`, `corpus_tables` and `corpus_streams` and `empty_gold_json` do too.
    - **What a stop at the request cap gives.** The stop is [`ReachedBound::RequestWork`](../../engine/arclog/src/eval/values.rs) of kind `RequestWork`. It is no assignment's stop: it ends the walk it stops, and each charge after it stops too, so the rest of the request stops at once. No cache keeps an entry or a mask that it cut short. Where the call gives a value, the stop stands in it as a stop at a cap stands: a stopped relation, `Consistency::Stopped` with the stop, the `stop` of the rows or projections of a body, a table with no TableHash and the reason `RequestWork`, a stopped table of `gold_tables`. Where the call gives no value for a stop, the mask of `admissible`, `admits` and `step` admits the tokens whose check a stop left open, as a stop at the table work cap does, and the decode keeps no mask of that state. A stop at the request byte budget stands in each of these places as a stop at the request work cap does, with the reason `RequestBytes`.
    - **Byte charges.** A request and an assignment each count the bytes that they hold at a time. A site that allocates charges the bytes of the allocation, by the sizes of its rows and columns, and a site that frees credits them: the rows a join holds are charged as they come, an export's buffers are charged where it reserves them and credited where the buffer drops, and the build of an index and the walk of a product hold their bytes while they run. A computation that a cache records (the evaluation of an entry, a nested search, a mask) frees at its end all but the bytes that its value holds: the scratch of the computation and the values of the entries it read. The value of a relation holds the bytes of its rows. An assignment that ends frees what it still holds in the request, but the buffers that an allocation of the session owns, which the owner frees where it drops. The bytes held depend on the program, the call and the settings alone, and not on what the session held, on the allocator, on the machine or on the thread.
    - **A hit holds the bytes of its entry.** An entry records, beside its work, the most bytes its computation held and the bytes its value holds. A hit in a cache, in a memo or in the result cache checks the budgets against the most that the entry's computation held, as a computation with empty caches does, and then holds the bytes that the entry's value holds, as the computation did; a hit whose peak would take a budget past its cap is no hit, as for work. A sum of every allocation of a request is no such count, and no budget uses it.
    - **Request byte budget.** Default: 2^32 bytes, provisional: the data of the gold programs' requests sets it, as it does the request work cap. A request that holds more bytes than it passes the budget, and stops, as a request at the request work cap does: the stop is [`ReachedBound::RequestBytes`](../../engine/arclog/src/eval/values.rs) of kind `RequestBytes`, it is no assignment's stop, it ends the walk it stops, and each charge after it stops too. The cache keeps no entry or mask that it cut short. The buffers of the rows that a call exports (`rows=True`) count in the request's bytes held, so this budget is the only room test of such a call. A call that leaves out tables at a stop of the request raises `arclog.BudgetReached` (see Counters below), and `successor_tables` gives the candidate the kind `exhausted`. The summary and the export of each assignment of a table hold their bytes under an assignment's byte budget too, so an assignment that holds more than that budget has no TableHash, with the reason `AssignmentBytes`, and its entries stopped, and the table's other assignments go on.
    - **Assignment byte budget.** Default: 2^31 bytes, provisional: the most bytes held at the true assignment of a gold program, with a margin, sets it. An assignment that holds more bytes than it stops alone, as an assignment stops at the table work cap: the stop is [`ReachedBound::AssignmentBytes`](../../engine/arclog/src/eval/values.rs) of kind `AssignmentBytes`, it rejects that assignment, and the other assignments go on with budgets of their own.
    - **Candidate byte budget.** Default: 2^31 bytes (`candidate_byte_cap`), provisional: the most bytes that one candidate's evaluation and export hold on the gold programs, with a margin, sets it. Each candidate of `successor_tables` has a request of its own ([`RequestWork::candidate`](../../engine/arclog/src/eval/values.rs)) with this byte budget in place of the request's. It is one constant: it does not depend on the order or the count of the candidates. A candidate that passes it is `exhausted` alone, and the others go on.
    - **Call guard.** A call that evaluates several candidates (`successor_tables`, `atom_tables`) has a request of its own, which counts the work that the candidates share, such as the evaluation of the body in progress and its export, and the work of no candidate. Each candidate runs in a request of its own ([`RequestWork::candidate`](../../engine/arclog/src/eval/values.rs)), under the request work cap and the candidate byte budget, and that request counts the candidate's step, its consistency test included, its evaluation, its summary and its export. An atom of `atom_tables` that passes either bound has no table and its place is in the list of atoms over the cap. The work of a candidate adds to no other candidate's count and to no count of the call's request, so a candidate is cut for its own size alone: a candidate whose step, evaluation or export passes a bound of its own is `exhausted`, and the tables of every other candidate are as they are with no such candidate. The call's request is an outer guard. Where its work passes the request work cap, or the bytes it holds pass the request byte budget, the call raises `arclog.CallGuardExhausted`, a subclass of `arclog.EngineFault`, with the attributes `bound`, the `StopKind` (`RequestWork` or `RequestBytes`), and `cap`, and gives no table of any candidate and cuts none. `arclog.request_stats()` gives the call's request, so its `work` and `bytes` are those of the shared work, and the gauges are shared by the candidates, so a gauge's `max` and `hits` cover every candidate. The effort guard is a bound over the whole call too, and a request past it raises `arclog.EffortExhausted` first. A call that stays under every bound gives the tables that it gives whatever the other candidates are.
    - **Resident bytes of a call's exports.** Default: 2^30 bytes (`spill_resident_bytes`). A call that keeps the exports of several candidates (`successor_tables`) keeps their cells in memory up to this bound. The cells of a later candidate wait on disk as zstd frames in a folder under the caller's `spill_dir`, or under the system's temporary folder, and `CaseTables.values` reads them back at each access, unchanged. The setting moves bytes and changes no result, no stop and no count. The folder is removed with its files when the last result of the call drops; a write that fails leaves the cells in memory.
    - **Session byte bound.** The bound on the bytes a session holds makes the session evict. Past the bound, a check evicts the entries of the result cache, and where the cache cannot give the bytes, the other caches of the session. It never stops or changes a request: no stop, no count, no gauge of a request and no value depends on which requests ran before, since a hit holds what its entry recorded and an evicted entry is computed again with the same charges. The gauge `session_bytes` records the largest bytes at a check, and has no hits.
    - **Mutation node cap.** Default: 2^30 search nodes, which no feasible search reaches, so a replay of the corpus runs a draw's search to the end of its finite domain; the synthesis settings give 2^17. Provisional: the nodes that a draw's search reaches on the gold programs set it. The search of the mutation draw has no other work cap, and it reads no clock. A search that reaches the cap ends with the candidates it has, and counts `domain_search_stopped:NodeCap`.
    - **Counters.** Each request keeps, for each capped quantity, the largest value it reached, the cap it ran under and the times it passed the cap. `arclog.request_stats()` gives them for the request of the last call on the thread, as `{work, cap, bytes, byte_cap, effort, effort_cap, gauges: {name: {max, cap, hits}}}`. `arclog.registry_text()` gives the registry text that `engine/arclog/src/registry.rs` embeds as `REGISTRY_TEXT`, from the build that holds the other names. A call that leaves out a table, other than a table of a candidate of a call that evaluates several, at a bound of its request raises `arclog.BudgetReached`, not a `RuntimeError`: its `bound` is the `StopKind` (`RequestWork` or `RequestBytes`), its `cap` the bound's cap and its `tables` the tables left out, each by its relation and the path of its form; the same call stops again. A counter is a load and a compare while no new largest value comes, and each is noted once per batch or per evaluation. Ground truth reaches no cap, so a hit marks a program that is probably wrong or a cap that is. These are the gauges:
        - `request_work`: the request work, the content count.
        - `effort`: the bindings that the request's searches tried, under the effort guard. A hit is a fault: the call raises `arclog.EffortExhausted`.
        - `table_work`: the table work of an observation, or of one step in the walk of a table's assignments.
        - `prefix_content`: the content of one prefix check, under the prefix content cap. A hit is a check left open: its token is admitted.
        - `bindings` and `rows`: the bindings and the rows of an evaluation.
        - `assignments`: the assignments of a walk. `models` and `nodes`: the models of an enumeration and the nodes of a constraint search.
        - `projection_rows` and `projection_steps`: the rows of a projection read and the steps of a completion search.
        - `request_bytes`: the most bytes a request held at a time, under the request byte budget. `assignment_bytes`: the most bytes one assignment held at a time, under the assignment byte budget.
        - `session_bytes`: the session's bytes at a check, under the bound at which the session evicts. A study request runs under `TABLE_BYTES`, which the gauge gives as its cap. The gauge has no hits.
        - `study_row_work`: the bindings of one assignment of one table that a study request counts under `TABLE_ROW_WORK`.
        - `pair_comparisons`: the row comparisons of one study pair, under the table work cap.
        - `compared_rows`: the rows of the larger of two relations that a comparison of successor tables reads row by row, under `COMPARED_ROWS`.
        - `atom_states`: the states that the walk of `Decoder::atoms` checked, under its `limit`. A hit is a truncation.
        - `labeling_nodes`: the search nodes of the canonical labeling of one label scope, under the labeling cap, 1000, provisional: the largest count over the gold requests sets it, with the margin of the other caps. A search past the cap is a hit, and the form is refused, not stopped: the check refuses it, the decode refuses the token that completes it with `Refusal::LabelingBound`, and `query_hash`, `query_bytes` and `written_query_hash` raise `arclog.LabelingBoundReached`, whose `bound` is `LabelingNodes` and whose `cap` is the cap. A form past the cap has no hash. A search runs with no limits at hand and a node keeps its result, so this counter is the process's and counts a search at the request that ran it: it runs from the begin of the latest request to the begin of the next, and a request on another thread that overlaps shares it.
        - `box_margin`: the largest margin of a Box that a term packed, in the bits a signed byte holds (a margin `m` takes `m` for `m >= 0` and `-m - 1` for `m < 0`), under 127. A term packs a Box with no limits at hand, so this counter is the process's and not the request's: it runs from the begin of the latest request to the begin of the next, and a request on another thread that overlaps shares it.
    - **Caps with no engine counter.**
        - The vocabulary caps are counted per gold stream by `engine/arclog-py/gold_request_work.py`, from the program and its tokens.
        - The study filters' `max_work` is counted in the result of each filter: `work` is the units counted and `work_cap` is `max_work`. A stop at it is the reason `stopped:Work`. A filter has no clock, so the stop depends on its inputs alone.
        - `PATH_MASKS`, `PREFIX_KEY_BYTES`, `PER_KEY` and `MADE_LABELS` size caches. They change the speed and no result, so they have no counter and no gold maximum.
    - **Determinism of the count.** Each entry of a cache keeps its cost: the units that its computation charged to the request, the bindings that it charged to the observation's count, the most bytes that it held and the bytes its value holds, in the request and in the assignment's budget, and the largest counts that its evaluations noted. A hit charges that cost, in the request, in the observation's count, in the gauges and in the session's largest counts. The cost of an entry holds the costs of the entries that its computation hit, so it is the cost of the computation with every cache empty and is a function of the entry alone. Each cache that skips charged work does this: the result cache, a computation that another thread ended while this one waited for it, the kept computations of the liveness walks, the kept domains of a `DET`, the kept walks of the parts of a program's `DET`s, the verdicts of the written `DET`s' admission, the masks of `admissible`, and the rows of the branches of unions. The code gives such an entry as a `Hit`, whose value comes out only through `Hit::charge` or `Hit::charge_if`: they charge the recorded cost, and give no value where the cost does not fit or the entry does not replay, and `Cost` has no other charge. The bytes of the build of an index are charged at each use of the index, whether or not the relation held it, and its rows are effort. Then the count of a request, its gauges and its stop at the cap are a function of the program and the call, and not of the earlier calls, of the cache's byte bound and its evictions, or of the thread that filled an entry first, so `step_batch` gives the count of each decode that its step gives alone. A hit whose cost would take a count or the bytes held past a cap is no hit: the entry is computed again, so the computation stops at the charge where it stops with every cache empty, and the stop, the count and the gauges at the stop are those of that computation. A count is what a call counts with empty caches, so a call that reads a warm cache counts more than it did when a hit charged nothing. What the cost does not cover: a decode's step that takes the state a held mask gives in place of a check, `admits`, which checks its tokens whatever the decode holds, and an entry that two relations of the same rows but of different parts read, whose work depends on the parts. `arclog-cli count-determinism` runs one request with cold caches and after other requests, under several caps, and on several threads that share a session, and reports each difference.
    - **No clock.** No query, mask, step, table, walk or search of a decode reads a clock, so a stop does not depend on the machine's speed, and the tables are the same on a pod and on a laptop. These caps bound the paths:
        - The evaluation of a query: the binding cap and the row cap, for each query.
        - A request: the request work cap and the request byte budget, over every query and walk of it, and the effort guard, which stops a runaway as a fault.
        - An observation, the extents: the table work cap.
        - The prefix check of the mask's walk at a `DET` close: the prefix content cap, and the effort guard.
        - An assignment of a table's walk: the table work cap and the assignment byte budget, each counted from none at the assignment.
        - The search of the mutation draw: the mutation node cap.
        - The joint assignments of a prefix, the models of a case and the nodes of the constraint search: the assignment cap, the model cap and the node cap.
        - A projection: the projection cap and the binding cap. The completion search of an input's head: the step bound of the search, 4096.
- The candidates of a train pair are not a setting. A run has the candidates of its task data, as [Candidates](cases.md#candidates) gives, so a decode on Kaggle task data is open-world and a decode on the task data of this repo is closed-world.

## Switches

- A switch has a reason to take two values where one of these holds: it bounds time, work or memory; it chooses what a call reports or costs, and changes no token that a decode admits; it names a diagnosis or a study that a run of the writer does not make; it sets a thread count or an input path. A switch that changes what the grammar admits has one value, since a path with a looser grammar than the model's writer accepts streams that the writer never writes.
- Each decode applies the static prune of `engine/arclog/src/program/static_prune.rs`, offers every proposer of the registry as a token, the type-domain proposers among them, and gives the grids at the model cap to its consistency tests and attempts, as [Enumeration and its stops](cases.md#enumeration-and-its-stops) gives. The readback of a program, the `verdicts` tool, the corpus replays and the Python bindings apply them too. The Python `DecodeSettings` still takes `proposer_tokens`, `type_domain_proposers`, `static_pruning` and `grids_at_model_cap` as keyword arguments, so that a record of settings and code that passes them still run. Each takes `True` or `None`, and `False` raises `ValueError`. Each reads as `True`.
- Decode settings that remain, each with its reason:
    - Mutation node cap: guards the search of the mutation draw, which has no other work cap.
    - Request byte budget and assignment byte budget: bound the bytes a request and an assignment hold at a time, which no work cap bounds.
    - Request work cap: bounds the content of a request, which no cap of one query bounds.
    - Effort guard: bounds the bindings that a request's searches try, which no content count bounds. Its stop is a fault.
    - Row cap, binding cap, projection cap, table work cap and prefix content cap: bound the work of an evaluation, a projection search, an observation and a prefix check.
    - Vocabulary caps: bound the slots of the vocabulary, which fix its size. A model reads one size, so a size changes with a new model.
    - Rule tests: cost of the search for the grid columns at each `RULE` end. They change no token, and the rollout export asks for their verdicts as labels.
    - Check copies: a diagnosis that reports the rows that a construction does not copy. It changes no token.
- Environment variables that remain:
    - `ARCLOG_THREADS`: the thread count of a corpus run.
    - `ARCLOG_STATS`: the switch of the per-phase timing, which costs time.
    - `ARCLOG_PROJECTION_MEASURE`: a diagnosis that runs the search of `Decoder::legacy_completes_projection` beside the exact one and prints the verdicts that differ.
    - `ARCLOG_JOIN_MEASURE`: a diagnosis that prints to the standard error, for each clause that did work, its form path, node count, work and time. It costs time and changes no result.
    - `GOLD_PRUNE_SCOPE`, `GOLD_PRUNE_PYTHON`, `GOLD_PRUNE_MANIFEST`, `GOLD_PRUNE_OUTPUT`, `GOLD_PRUNE_ACQUISITION_KEY`: the scope and the files of the gold normalization audit.
    - `ARCLOG_IDENTITY_PAIRS`, `ARCLOG_TASKS`, `ARCLOG_PAIR_FILTER`, `ARCLOG_TABLE_WORK_CAP`, `ARCLOG_SHOW`, `ARCLOG_SHOW_TASK`, `STORED_PRUNE_FIXTURES`: the input paths and the filter of a test or audit.
    - `GENERATE_*`, `ATOM_*`, `TIME_TABLES` and `TIME_SHAPES`: the inputs, the time bounds and the diagnosis modes of the `feature-encoder` tool.
- Cargo features that remain: `extension-module` links the Python extension as maturin needs, and `admissible-reference` builds the exhaustive oracle that the admissible workload check compares with.
- Keyword arguments of the Python bindings that remain:
    - `step_cap`, `max_work`, `limit`: bounds.
    - `bodies`, `rows`, `summaries`, `by_assignment`, `by_program`, `before`, `top_k`, `compact`, `observation_start`, `max_assignments`, `units`, `task_ids`, `all_cases`, `started`, `ended`, `progress`: choose what a call builds, reports or runs over, and so its cost.
    - `copies`: the check copies diagnosis, for the table exports.
    - `evaluate` of `Session.decoder`: with `False` the decode runs the grammar, the checks and the static prune and evaluates nothing, so a stream check costs less. It refuses no token for want of an evaluation, so it admits streams that the full decode refuses, and a verdict about a model's stream comes from the full decode.
    - `reference` of `Decoder.atoms`: runs the reference walk that checks the candidate atoms.
    - `prefix`: reads the files as a prefix of a solver.
    - `survey`, `normalize`, `restrictions`, `operation`: choose the report or the kind of edit of a study tool.
    - `task_level`, `capped`: facts of the table that a call describes, not switches.
- Flags of the command line tools that remain: `--no-mask` of `stream_round_trip` reads a program through the checks alone, to tell a fault of the checks from a fault of the mask, and `--no-rows` of `decode` leaves out the rows.

## Token mask

- One automaton, `Decoder`, reads the token stream of a program. Its state is the partial program so far and the clause in progress.
- `Decoder::new(task, session, registry, constructions, settings)` starts a decode over a task in a task session. These are the arguments of `Evaluation` in `engine/arclog/src/eval/mod.rs` less the program and its parameter values, which a decode builds and does not store, with the decode settings, which hold the caps, in place of its limits. The settings' caps are the session's caps, since a stored stop holds for every request of a session, as [The result cache](evaluator.md#the-result-cache) gives.
- `admissible()` gives the token mask: the tokens that may come next. A token is in the mask when every check of [Checker](reader.md#checker) holds on the partial program with that token.
- A check's prefix form refuses at the token where a refusal can no longer be undone:
    - The grammar, the scope, a site's columns, the pose rule, the pooled and lifted reads, the reads of an `Unfold` instance, an instance's input order, the type meet and the join over branches each refuse at the token that breaks them. A meet only narrows as sites are added, and a join only widens.
    - A missing instance input, the labels that an `OR` or an `IF` exports, and the checks of a `CHOOSE` form are decided at their form's `END`, so the mask can withhold only that `END`.
    - In the key body of an `INIT` form, a `^` label is refused once the form that holds it can export it in no completion, since only an atom at the key body's top level binds the key's column: at the `^` in a `NOT`, an `ALL`, a later branch of an `OR`, or the test or the else of an `IF`, and at the `ALSO` or the `END` that ends a later branch of an `OR`, or the else of an `IF`, where that branch or else does not name the label.
    - A label that only `(= *X 0)` types is bound by `0`, as the safety condition of [Negation and constraints](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#negation-and-constraints) gives. Inside an instance input, its type is the input's column at the column name of the head site that projects it. The comparison typing rule refuses a constraint on it only once no completion can type it `number`. Each relation of a decode has one clause, so a top-level `(= *X 0)` in a `RULE` or a `DET`, in a `CHOOSE` form's `from`, in a `RIGID_DET` form's `instance` clause, or in a clause of a `DEMO_RULE` other than its `&prediction` clause, is refused at its constraint.
- The checks of an `OUT` form, as [The OUT form](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#the-out-form) gives them, refuse at the token that breaks a rule: a conjunct that is no atom, a third atom, an atom of a relation that `OUT` does not read, a site that no label marked `^` alone completes, an atom or a site of a relation with no column of the type the next selected column has, and a label of another type than that column's. So inside an `OUT` form the mask admits only the tokens after which some completion passes, and withholds its `END` until the form selects two columns. The mask admits `OUT` wherever a form may open.
- The mask admits `(ONE)` on a `DET`, which makes its parameter open, as [Open parameters](#open-parameters) gives. It admits `ONE` marks on a `RULE`, and the derived reading of a parameter, `(RULE &x <proposer>)`.
- The mask refuses `DEMONSTRATED`, `DEMO_RULE`, `PREDICT` and `ALT_FORM` at every state, and so does `step`, as [Token stream](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#token-stream) gives. No setting admits them.
- The mask blocks none of the [second spellings](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#second-spellings), since that section gives them as suggestions and not rules of the stream.
- `step(token)` advances the state, and gives the tokens that the engine emits after that token: declarations, the names of inputs and of named clauses, column names and label echoes, as [Token stream](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#token-stream) gives, and, at the `END` of an `OUT` form of a decode that judges the train pairs, the consistency of its relation, and, under the rule tests, at the `END` of a `RULE` clause, the verdict of its relation, as [Decode interface](#decode-interface) gives.
- The vocabulary has fixed slots: the control tokens, the lib relations and components, the proposers, the fact relations, then one relation slot, one column slot and one label slot per index, up to the vocabulary caps of [Decode settings](#decode-settings), and last the control tokens `DEMONSTRATED`, `DEMO_RULE`, `PREDICT` and `ALT_FORM`. The proposer slots hold every proposer of the registry, the type-domain proposers among them.

## Checks moved from the lint

The checks of `src/arclog_convert/lint.py` that move to the engine in this step are the checks of the language that [Checker](reader.md#checker) does not build in step 1, by the code the lint reports:

- `=` and `0`: `head-zero`, `zero`, `zero-site`, `eq-order`, `eq-site`, `eq-mark`, `eq-same-list`.
- Labels and marks: `mark`, `remade`, `fresh-here`.
- One clause per relation: `clause-twice`.
- Instances: `broadcast-inside`, `key-site`.
- Picks: `one`, `one-place`, `choose`, `choose-no-never`, `choose-one`, `never-reads`.
- `RIGID_DET`: `rigid`, `rigid-part`.
- Proposers: `proposer`.
- The spelling of the program form, which no token stream can break, since the stream names nothing with text: `name`, `label-clash`, `wildcard`, `elide`, `field-case`.

The checks of solver structure, of the arcdata file, of `DEMONSTRATED` and of scene files move in [Tooling](tooling.md) or stay with the lint.

## Decode interface

- At any token, the decode can ask for the rows and counts of the body so far on every example. The result cache gives them from the body's entries. The caller chooses whether to ask after each clause, each atom or each site.
- A body so far that reads a chosen relation of more than one model, or an `Unfold` instance of more than one value, is evaluated at the first model of each such factor, in the canonical order, with no search for a second. The rows, counts, projections and body table of the body so far are those of that model. The first model is the first in the order of the options of a pick, as [Models](cases.md#models) gives. The `DET` reads of [Determination](determination.md) are not changed: a `DET` that reads such a factor is still a fault. Taking the first of several models is a choice among equals, and the contract's form for several models is to keep them all and average a feature over them, so this rule is a stopgap; enumerating the models of such a prefix is the principled change, to be sized when the writer's features at those prefixes matter.
- The body so far of an `INIT` form is its key body, or an input's body after the key body's conjuncts where the input is keyed, with each label that the key marks as a label of the body, as [Candidate atoms](#candidate-atoms) gives. Its rows, counts, projections and body table are those of any body so far, at the side that the form has with what it reads so far.
- The decode also gives the maximal projections of the body so far: the body's projections, without repeated rows, onto the largest sets of its columns whose projection holds at most the projection cap of [Decode settings](#decode-settings) on every example, at every assignment. A body under the cap is its one projection, and a set of no columns is no projection. A read whose columns lie inside a projection sees the rows it sees on the body, since a relation is a set. The search for the projections counts the rows it reads of each value of the body, one example at one assignment, under the binding cap.
- When an `OUT` form ends, the engine reports whether it is consistent: its output set, read at the form's columns as [The output of the `OUT` form](cases.md#the-output-of-the-out-form) gives, solves the train pairs, as [Solves](cases.md#solves) gives for the run's candidates. The decode ends at the first `OUT` form that is consistent, and at nothing else, whatever the settings. After an `OUT` form that is not consistent, the decode admits what it admits after any finished form. A `DET` relation is not tested.
- A prefix that reads a `DET` whose domain holds several values is evaluated at every assignment, as [Determination with no stored value](determination.md#determination-with-no-stored-value) gives. The decode adds the test at an `OUT` form's end: the consistency test runs per assignment, and the form keeps the consistent assignments, those under which it solves every train pair. Those are the values that the filter of [`DET`](determination.md#det) gives a program with that `OUT` form.
- A `ONE` or a `CHOOSE` form whose rows are not all written is evaluated as its plain body, whose rows bound the rows of each of its models. No clause reads the pick before the form ends, since a clause reads only relations declared before it. At its `END`, its models are built as [Models](cases.md#models) gives.
- The assignments of a prefix run under the assignment cap, its models under the model cap and the node cap, and its evaluations under the row cap and the binding cap of [Decode settings](#decode-settings). An evaluation stop rejects its assignment and the walk continues: a stop at the row cap, the binding cap, the model cap or the node cap, a margin or a variable with no values, a stop at the table work cap, and a stop at the assignment byte budget, which counts the bytes the assignment charges, as [Byte charges](#decode-settings) gives, and so depends on that assignment alone; every table walk of a decode, with or without an observation budget, has one ([`ReachedBound::is_assignment`](../../engine/arclog/src/eval/values.rs)). Each step of the walk of a table, a domain or the leaf of an assignment, counts its table work and its bytes in counts of its own under the caps, which add to the observation's count without that count's cap, so one assignment that passes a cap loses its own rows and the next step counts from none. The request work cap, the request byte budget, the assignment cap, a cancellation and a stop at the session's byte bound end the walk. The walk records its first stop. A consistency test with kept assignments can give `Consistent` beside that stop; with none, it gives `Stopped` where a stop occurred and `NotConsistent` otherwise. The trace records the stop.
- The `END`, `SET` or `ONE` that closes a `DET` or a `RIGID_DET` is in the mask where a live assignment gives each written `DET` a value and each model pick a model. The walk reads the evaluation caps and a budget of the prefix content cap of [Decode settings](#decode-settings). An evaluation stop rejects its path and the walk visits the next value. A live leaf admits the token even when another path stopped. With no live leaf, a recorded stop at the assignment cap, a margin or a variable with no values refuses the token; a stop at a resource bound (the row cap, the binding cap, the model cap, the node cap, the table work cap, the prefix content cap, the request work cap or a byte budget) admits it, since the time, rows and bytes spent observing the walk establish no property of the query, and the form's end decides. A stop at the effort guard is a fault of the call. The steps after a `DET` whose values no later step reads are the same at each value, so the walk takes them once and counts the leaves of that taking for each value.
- A `DET` that an `OUT` form reads and that takes more than one value over the consistent assignments is tested as [Open parameters](#open-parameters) gives, with `(ONE)` or without it. For a solver, such a `DET` without `(ONE)` is an error, as [`DET`](determination.md#det) gives. The rule tests of [Decode settings](#decode-settings) keep `SeveralValues` for such a `DET` at a `RULE` end, for the clause's relation, with underdetermined grid columns among their verdicts.
- An extension of a prefix whose part reached the row cap is evaluated from its own reads, as [The result cache](evaluator.md#the-result-cache) gives, since a site added to a positive atom can bring the part under the cap.
- `fork()` is cheap. The state is a persistent data structure and the cache is shared, so a beam of decodes costs the states alone.
- A batch form steps many decodes at once on the thread pool.

## Candidate atoms

A model that scores a whole atom as one action reads two calls.

- `Decoder::candidate_atoms` gives the atoms that may come next at a state. An atom is a relation, with `NOT` first where one stands before it, with one value at each site, its sites in ascending order of their columns, and the fields of a record in ascending order, where the mask after its last site admits a token that closes it.
    - The atoms come in one order: at each place the tokens in ascending order of their ids, and an atom before the atoms that add sites to it.
    - The walk asks the checks once for each shape of an atom, and the atoms of one shape share the verdict. A shape holds the atom's relation, its columns and values, what stands on the stack, and whether the atom repeats a conjunct of the body. A label stands in a shape as its type, whether it is bound, whether a key marks it, whether a `NOT` holds it and whether an earlier site of the atom holds it. It stands as its id, with the verdicts held for that form alone, where a conjunct of the body gives it no type, where a type rule of the relation reads whether two labels stand in one grid, or where it stands outside the scope of the atom's body: in a branch of an `OR` alone, in another body alone, or in the key body of a keyed input with no mark of the key.
    - In a body of an `INIT` form, what stands on the stack holds the component, the position of the input and the form's other bodies, other than the key body that a keyed input reads, whose labels have shapes. A label of another body stands as its id.
    - The verdicts of lib and fact relations read no program and no task, so the sessions of a process share them, other than a place whose closing token is no relation's. Those of a program's relations stand with the state's finished forms. `arclog.atom_verdicts` gives the shared verdicts as bytes, and `arclog.add_atom_verdicts` adds them in a process of the same build, so that its walks start warm. It refuses the bytes of another build, whose checks may differ. A verdict's key holds the settings that a check reads, so one file holds the verdicts of several settings.
    - `candidate_atoms_reference` gives the same list from a whole mask at each token, for a check of the walk.
- `atom_tables` in `arclog_tables` gives the summary of the table of each atom: the body in progress with that atom as a closed conjunct, at each assignment.
    - The body in progress of an `INIT` form is its key body, or an input's body after the key body's conjuncts where the input is keyed, with each label that the key marks as a label of the body. Its side is the side of the form with what it reads so far. Where the engine has named a body and no token has opened it, the body in progress is that body.
    - The body in progress in a branch of an `OR` is a body of its own: the conjuncts around the `OR`, then the conjuncts of the branch so far, with the branch's labels as columns. It reads no other branch.
    - A table holds the columns of the body, then the labels that the atom adds, in the order of its sites. Its features read that order: `suit` reads the first columns of a type.
    - Atoms with equal tables share one summary, and an atom holds the place of its summary. A summary holds the features at each assignment, and a hash of the rows at each assignment ([`Relation::rows_hash`](../../engine/arclog/src/relations.rs)), which tells equal rows at the same columns. It is no `TableHash`, and a program can hold the same columns in another order, so it compares the assignments of one atom alone.
    - An atom has no summary where no clause, `CHOOSE` or `INIT` body is in progress, where the check refuses the closed body, or where the atom's work passes its cap. The result lists the atoms whose work passed the cap.
    - The rows of an atom come from a join of its relation to the rows of the parts of the body that it reads, with no program made for the atom. An atom that this join does not read takes a program of its own: an atom in an `IF`, an `ALL` or a `NOT` of a form, a site of a record, a column with no fixed scalar type, a type rule that reads whether two labels stand in one grid, a read of a `DET` that the body does not read, a read that changes the side of an `INIT` form, and a relation per example read from a body of the task.
    - An atom that reads one part of the body's rows, at values that satisfy a mode of its relation, reads that part through groups: the rows with equal values at the atom's bound columns. The atom reads its relation once for each group, and the atoms that fill the same sites from the same groups share what they read, an atom and its `NOT` among them. The hash of the atom's table comes from sums over the groups, and the rows of a table are made where no earlier atom has that hash.
    - The session holds the result of the call by the state and its list of atoms, up to a bound on its bytes, since a search reaches a state of a shared prefix many times.
    - No deadline applies, and the summary holds no `DET`s of an assignment.

## Open parameters

- A `DET` with `(ONE)` in a synthesized program is an open parameter, under the readings of [Open values](cases.md#open-values) that a program with one case and no alt can hold: a `DET` whose body ends in `(ONE)`, whose models are its open values, and a `DET` by a proposer with `(ONE)`, whose open values the consistent assignments give.
- A `DET` without `(ONE)` in a candidate program is an open parameter of that second reading, at an `OUT` form's end, where it takes more than one value over the consistent assignments. A solver's author can pin such a value, and a candidate has no author, so under open-world candidates the values that the train pairs leave stay open.
- With several `DET`s of the second reading, the open values of each are the projection onto it of the consistent assignments, and the program runs over the consistent assignments, which are joint, and not over the product of the `DET`s' domains. A solver's open values stay one set per parameter, as [Models](cases.md#models) gives; no solver holds two open `DET`s with a body, so the joint reading differs from the product for a decoded program alone.
- The program reads as M_open over those values, as [Open parameters](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#open-parameters) gives: on an input, its output is the union of the grids its assignments give, and it is undefined where it is undefined at one of them.
- The open values are models of the case, as [Models](cases.md#models) gives, so M_open reads the output set of the `OUT` form at each consistent assignment, at the form's columns. The test at each assignment alone serves the filter of [`DET`](determination.md#det).
- At an `OUT` form's end, the form is consistent where M_open solves every train pair, as [Solves](cases.md#solves) gives for the run's candidates: under closed-world candidates the union is one candidate of the pair, and under open-world candidates it holds at most two grids, one of them the given output. On Kaggle, M_open's grids on a test input are its attempts, as [Solves](cases.md#solves) gives.
- The consistent assignments are evaluated already, as [Decode interface](#decode-interface) gives, so M_open over them reads their output sets and runs no evaluation of its own. M_open adds the evaluation of each consistent assignment on the test inputs, under the assignment cap of [Decode settings](#decode-settings).

## Factorized bodies

- A conjunction of atoms, a whole body or a part of one, has an entry in the result cache, keyed by its content. That includes partial atoms. Each entry is evaluated from its own reads by one join plan. Equal entries reuse their value, and other cached bodies do not select a different join for it.
- The cache keeps the parts of each body, so that a search that adds or removes any atom finds its entries. A part is a connected component of the body's atoms, where two atoms are connected when they share a variable. Each part has its own entry, and the body's rows are the product of its parts' rows.
- A part's entry holds its rows in two parts where a Position column splits its atoms: the atoms of one set read the column only at columns of algebra relations that read a Position through its frame alone, such as `box_extent`'s `P`, and the other set binds it. Where the values the other set gives the column have one frame, the first set is joined once, at one of them, and the entry's rows are the product of the two sets' rows. The row cap counts the rows that each of the two sets holds, and no row of the product is built.
- A part is a subset of the body's atoms, and not a prefix. So the same entries serve decoding, which adds each atom after the last, and a search that adds or removes any atom.
- A finished clause's entry is the entry of its whole body, whatever order built it. The join order of a run with no decode is the evaluator's, and every order gives the same rows.
- The count of a body's rows is the product of its parts' counts, and the body is empty when any part is empty. So pruning a decode on a count or on emptiness builds no product.
- A read that walks the product of a relation of more than one part, such as the domain of a `DET`, the input of an instance, the lifted form of a relation per example or the union of a relation's forms, counts each row of the product as content, before it builds any of them, against the binding cap of [Decode settings](#decode-settings). The index of an atom over the relation counts the combinations of the parts that hold its columns, each part read by its distinct projections onto those columns. A bounded observation also charges its own work budget. The join of each part counted that part's rows, and no join counts their product, so a product past a cap stops the read, and builds no row. A prune reads the positive atoms at the top level of the body and the complete forms alone, as [Programs as values](reader.md#programs-as-values) gives.
- The generic join of [Joins](evaluator.md#joins) does the last join from the parts' indexes.
- A negated atom whose variables lie in two parts links those parts, and it is checked when they are joined.

## Traces

- The engine keeps a trace of each decode and of each run: an append-only list of records, one per step, in the order of the steps.
    - In a decode, a step is one token. Its record holds the token; whether the language model or the engine emitted it; what the engine computed at that step, such as the rows and counts the caller asked for, the consistency test at an `OUT` form's end and, under the rule tests, the verdict at a `RULE` clause's end; and, where the caller attaches them, the language model's top-k tokens with their probabilities. The record also holds the count of the output sets that the step's tests ended at the model cap, `capped_sets`. Python reads the sum of the counts as `Decoder.capped_sets`, the count of one test as the `capped_sets` of its `Consistency`, and the count of the attempts as the second value of `Decoder.attempts_counted()`.
    - In a run, a step is the evaluation of one relation of one case on one input. Its record holds the row count, the models enumerated, the values determined, and a stop at a cap.
- Three layers are expected to change as the synthesis work iterates: the observations given to the language model at a step, the rendering of the trace, and the layout of the vocabulary, of which the tokens of [Token stream](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#token-stream) are the starting layout. Each sits over the trace, and no part of the evaluator depends on it.
- A renderer is one such layer. The form of [Trace form](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#trace-form) is one renderer. The program form is the trace form under the rewrites of [From trace form to program form](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#from-trace-form-to-program-form). The synthesis code decides which top-k tokens the trace form shows and how it shows a decision, and those choices are the renderer's.
