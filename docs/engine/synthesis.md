# Synthesis

The step of the [build order](overview.md#build-order) after [Determination](determination.md). It builds token ids and the vocabulary, a writer from a case program to its token stream, the decoder and its token mask, the prefix forms of the checks and the checks of the language that [Checker](reader.md#checker) leaves to the Python lint, the traces, entries in the result cache for parts of a body, the Python package, the wheel, and a run on Kaggle. It rests on [Token stream](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#token-stream) and [Trace form](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#trace-form).

The step ends when each of these holds, or the difference is reported as a fault, as the Principles requirement of [Requirements](overview.md#requirements) gives:

- The decoder admits the token stream that the writer gives each case program of `gen/`, in full, or through the `END` of its first `OUT` form that holds, as [Decode interface](#decode-interface) gives, with the tokens after it no part of the program the decode reads. A case program that holds a fault of its solver or of the conversion that [Determination](determination.md) names is reported as that fault.
- At the `END` of each such stream's `OUT` form, the consistency result equals the solves outcome of the case at the values that [Determination](determination.md) determines.
- The rows of each prefix of those streams equal the rows that the evaluator gives the prefix's sub-body.
- The concept case program of aba27056, run in the Kaggle notebook on its task with the test output held out, gives one attempt on its test input, and that attempt is the test output.

## Decode settings

- The decode settings are one value, which a decode reads, and each setting has a default. The mask, the consistency test, the rule tests or the tables of [Decode interface](#decode-interface) read a setting, and no other part of the design depends on its value, so the synthesis work can change one with no change to the design.
    - **Proposer tokens.** The mask offers the proposers as tokens, or the program writes each determination body as clauses. Default: the mask offers them. The mask reads it.
    - **Type-domain proposers.** The mask offers the type-domain proposers of [Proposers](determination.md#proposers), or not. Default: it offers them. The mask reads it.
    - **Vocabulary caps.** The relation, column and label slots of [Token mask](#token-mask). Default: the corpus's maxima with a margin. The largest counts over the 942 concept cases of `gen/` that give a stream are 201 relations declared (a64e4611), 23 column positions (a57f2f04) and 28 labels of one form (28a6681f). Each cap is the least power of two at or above twice its count: 512, 64 and 64. The margin is provisional: no decode shows how far a synthesized program goes past the corpus. The mask reads them.
    - **Assignment cap.** Default: the cap of [`DET`](determination.md#det). The consistency test reads it, at each prefix.
    - **Model cap and node cap.** Default: the caps of [The constraint module](cases.md#the-constraint-module). The consistency test reads them, at each prefix.
    - **Row cap and binding cap.** Default: the caps of [Joins](evaluator.md#joins). One query evaluates a clause, finished or in progress, on one example at one assignment. Its evaluations read these caps on every path, including verification, table acquisition, determination, the mask and attempts. The binding count of an evaluation is independent of other cached entries and of the assignment order. No count sums the queries of a program against either cap.
    - **Projection cap.** Default: 10,000 rows, provisional: no measurement yet gives the smallest cap at which each read of the gold solvers that lies outside every maximal projection is a pairing inside a search over candidates. The maximal projections of [Decode interface](#decode-interface) read it.
    - **Table work cap.** Default: 2^20 bindings; provisional. This budgets observations: extents and the mask's walk at a `DET` close. Relation and body queries keep a work count with no total cap across examples or assignments. An extent derivation has its own count, under this setting times the number of table assignments, with an empty assignment domain counted as one. A stop of an extent derivation at that budget or an evaluation bound leaves the rows available and records the stop. The row comparisons of a study have their own bounded counts. Observation evaluations hold table-work stops by result-cache key for later bounded observations of the decode; row queries do not read those stops.
    - **Rule tests.** Default: off. With it off, no `RULE` end runs a test of its relation. With it on, the `END` of each `RULE` clause gives, beside the step's other results, the verdict of the test of the clause's relation: the search for one Position column and one Color column of the relation, its grid columns, under which the grids, read model by model, solve every train pair, as [Solves](cases.md#solves) gives for the run's candidates, at each joint assignment of the `DET`s the relation reads. The verdicts are `Consistent`, `NotConsistent` where no pair of columns solves the pairs or the assignments keep none, `SeveralValues` where a `DET` without `(ONE)` takes more than one value over the consistent assignments, `Fault`, where two pairs of columns solve the pairs and give different output sets on an input of the task, which makes the grid columns underdetermined, or a run faults, and `Stopped` at a cap or a deadline. The verdict ends no decode, makes no relation the output, and changes no mask, so the setting changes no token that a decode admits and no verdict at an `OUT` form. The cost of the search is that of [The grid columns at each clause's end](performance.md#the-grid-columns-at-each-clauses-end). The consistency test of the `OUT` form reads none of it. The decode reads the setting at a `RULE` clause's end.
    - **Grids at the model cap.** Default: on. With it on, a case that reaches the model cap on an input gives the set of the models up to the cap, as [Enumeration and its stops](cases.md#enumeration-and-its-stops) gives, and the consistency tests and the attempts use that set. With it off, such a case has no output on that input, and the tests give `Stopped`. The consistency tests and the attempts read it. The `DET` admission and the determination read none of it. The name is `grids_at_model_cap` in the engine and in the Python `DecodeSettings`.
    - **Deadline.** Default: 10 s per candidate program, provisional: no Kaggle run yet gives the count of candidate programs per task. A replay of the corpus runs under none, so its tables and verdicts do not depend on the machine's load. In one decode, an evaluation of a result-cache entry that stops at the deadline is held, with its stop and the time it ran, in the decode's store of held stops. A later request of the decode that needs that entry, at the same result-cache key, with at most 1.1 times that time left to its own deadline (the factor is provisional), takes that stop, at the form that stopped, and evaluates nothing for it. A request with more time left evaluates the entry, and a stop at the deadline that it reaches holds the larger time. A fork of the decode shares the stops at the deadline with its decode and the forks of it, since those depend on the machine, and starts from the stops at the table work cap that its decode holds at the fork. The consistency test reads the deadline.
- The candidates of a train pair are not a setting. A run has the candidates of its task data, as [Candidates](cases.md#candidates) gives, so a decode on Kaggle task data is open-world and a decode on the task data of this repo is closed-world.

## Token mask

- One automaton, `Decoder`, reads the token stream of a program. Its state is the partial program so far and the clause in progress.
- `Decoder::new(task, session, registry, constructions, settings)` starts a decode over a task in a task session. These are the arguments of `Evaluation` in `engine/arclog/src/eval/mod.rs` less the program and its parameter values, which a decode builds and does not store, with the decode settings, which hold the caps and the deadline, in place of its limits. The settings' caps are the session's caps, since a stored stop holds for every request of a session, as [The result cache](evaluator.md#the-result-cache) gives.
- `admissible()` gives the token mask: the tokens that may come next. A token is in the mask when every check of [Checker](reader.md#checker) holds on the partial program with that token.
- A check's prefix form refuses at the token where a refusal can no longer be undone:
    - The grammar, the scope, a site's columns, the pose rule, the pooled and lifted reads, the reads of an `Unfold` instance, an instance's input order, the type meet and the join over branches each refuse at the token that breaks them. A meet only narrows as sites are added, and a join only widens.
    - A missing instance input, the labels that an `OR` or an `IF` exports, and the checks of a `CHOOSE` form are decided at their form's `END`, so the mask can withhold only that `END`.
    - In the key body of an `INIT` form, a `^` label is refused once the form that holds it can export it in no completion, since only an atom at the key body's top level binds the key's column: at the `^` in a `NOT`, an `ALL`, a later branch of an `OR`, or the test or the else of an `IF`, and at the `ALSO` or the `END` that ends a later branch of an `OR`, or the else of an `IF`, where that branch or else does not name the label.
    - A label that only `(= *X 0)` types is bound by `0`, as the safety condition of [Negation and constraints](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#negation-and-constraints) gives. Inside an instance input, its type is the input's column at the column name of the head site that projects it. The comparison typing rule refuses a constraint on it only once no completion can type it `number`. Each relation of a decode has one clause, so a top-level `(= *X 0)` in a `RULE` or a `DET` is refused at its constraint.
- The checks of an `OUT` form, as [The OUT form](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#the-out-form) gives them, refuse at the token that breaks a rule: a conjunct that is no atom, a third atom, an atom of a relation that `OUT` does not read, a site that no label marked `^` alone completes, an atom or a site of a relation with no column of the type the next selected column has, and a label of another type than that column's. So inside an `OUT` form the mask admits only the tokens after which some completion passes, and withholds its `END` until the form selects two columns. The mask admits `OUT` wherever a form may open.
- The mask admits `(ONE)` on a `DET`, which makes its parameter open, as [Open parameters](#open-parameters) gives. It admits `ONE` marks on a `RULE`, and the derived reading of a parameter, `(RULE &x <proposer>)`.
- Inside a `DEMONSTRATED` form each body takes the prefix checks of the same body at the top level, in its own label scope. The key and inputs are one label scope, each `DEMO_RULE` is one, and each form after `ALT_FORM` is one. A form of a `DEMO_RULE` ends at its own `END`, and the key and inputs end at the first `DEMO_RULE`. A read that a per-input form may not make, in the key, an input or a `&prediction` clause, is refused at the `END` of the `DEMONSTRATED` form, since the reads of the whole form decide its side.
- The mask blocks none of the [second spellings](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#second-spellings), since that section gives them as suggestions and not rules of the stream.
- `step(token)` advances the state, and gives the tokens that the engine emits after that token: declarations, the names of inputs and of named clauses, column names and label echoes, as [Token stream](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#token-stream) gives, and, at the `END` of an `OUT` form of a decode that judges the train pairs, the consistency of its relation, and, under the rule tests, at the `END` of a `RULE` clause, the verdict of its relation, as [Decode interface](#decode-interface) gives.
- The vocabulary has fixed slots: the control tokens, the lib relations and components, the proposers, the fact relations, then one relation slot, one column slot and one label slot per index, up to the vocabulary caps of [Decode settings](#decode-settings), and last the control tokens `DEMONSTRATED`, `DEMO_RULE`, `PREDICT` and `ALT_FORM`. The proposer slots, and the type-domain proposers among them, are those that the proposer tokens and the type-domain proposers settings offer.

## Checks moved from the lint

The checks of `src/arclog_convert/lint.py` that move to the engine in this step are the checks of the language that [Checker](reader.md#checker) does not build in step 1, by the code the lint reports:

- `=` and `0`: `head-zero`, `zero`, `zero-site`, `eq-order`, `eq-site`, `eq-mark`, `eq-same-list`.
- Labels and marks: `mark`, `remade`, `fresh-here`.
- One clause per relation: `clause-twice`.
- Instances: `broadcast-inside`, `key-site`.
- Picks: `one`, `one-place`, `choose`, `choose-no-never`, `choose-one`, `never-reads`.
- `RIGID_DET`: `rigid`, `rigid-part`.
- Proposers: `proposer`.
- The spelling of the program form, which no token stream can break, since the stream names nothing with text: `name`, `label-clash`, `wildcard`, `elide`, `field-case`.

The checks of solver structure, of the arcdata file, of `DEMONSTRATED` and of scene files move in [Tooling](tooling.md) or stay with the lint.

## Decode interface

- At any token, the decode can ask for the rows and counts of the body so far on every example. The result cache gives them from the body's entries. The caller chooses whether to ask after each clause, each atom or each site.
- The decode also gives the maximal projections of the body so far: the body's projections, without repeated rows, onto the largest sets of its columns whose projection holds at most the projection cap of [Decode settings](#decode-settings) on every example, at every assignment. A body under the cap is its one projection, and a set of no columns is no projection. A read whose columns lie inside a projection sees the rows it sees on the body, since a relation is a set. The search for the projections counts the rows it reads of each value of the body, one example at one assignment, under the binding cap.
- When an `OUT` form ends, the engine reports whether it is consistent: its output set, read at the form's columns as [The output of the `OUT` form](cases.md#the-output-of-the-out-form) gives, solves the train pairs, as [Solves](cases.md#solves) gives for the run's candidates. The decode ends at the first `OUT` form that is consistent, and at nothing else, whatever the settings. After an `OUT` form that is not consistent, the decode admits what it admits after any finished form. A `DET` relation is not tested.
- A prefix that reads a `DET` whose domain holds several values is evaluated at every assignment, as [Determination with no stored value](determination.md#determination-with-no-stored-value) gives. The decode adds the test at an `OUT` form's end: the consistency test runs per assignment, and the form keeps the consistent assignments, those under which it solves every train pair. Those are the values that the filter of [`DET`](determination.md#det) gives a program with that `OUT` form.
- A `ONE` or a `CHOOSE` form whose rows are not all written is evaluated as its plain body, whose rows bound the rows of each of its models. No clause reads the pick before the form ends, since a clause reads only relations declared before it. At its `END`, its models are built as [Models](cases.md#models) gives.
- The assignments of a prefix run under the assignment cap, its models under the model cap and the node cap, and its evaluations under the row cap and the binding cap of [Decode settings](#decode-settings). An evaluation stop rejects its assignment and the walk continues. The walk records its first stop. A consistency test with kept assignments can give `Consistent` beside that stop; with none, it gives `Stopped` where a stop occurred and `NotConsistent` otherwise. The trace records the stop.
- The `END`, `SET` or `ONE` that closes a `DET` or a `RIGID_DET` is in the mask where a live assignment gives each written `DET` a value and each model pick a model. The walk reads the evaluation caps and a budget of the table work cap of [Decode settings](#decode-settings). An evaluation stop rejects its path and the walk visits the next value. A live leaf admits the token even when another path stopped. With no live leaf, a recorded stop refuses the token; reaching the walk's work budget admits it, since the time spent observing the walk establishes no property of the query.
- A `DET` that an `OUT` form reads and that takes more than one value over the consistent assignments is tested as [Open parameters](#open-parameters) gives, with `(ONE)` or without it. For a solver, such a `DET` without `(ONE)` is an error, as [`DET`](determination.md#det) gives. The rule tests of [Decode settings](#decode-settings) keep `SeveralValues` for such a `DET` at a `RULE` end, for the clause's relation, with underdetermined grid columns among their verdicts.
- An extension of a prefix whose part reached the row cap is evaluated from its own reads, as [The result cache](evaluator.md#the-result-cache) gives, since a site added to a positive atom can bring the part under the cap.
- `fork()` is cheap. The state is a persistent data structure and the cache is shared, so a beam of decodes costs the states alone.
- A batch form steps many decodes at once on the thread pool.

## Candidate atoms

A model that scores a whole atom as one action reads two calls.

- `Decoder::candidate_atoms` gives the atoms that may come next at a state. An atom is a relation, with `NOT` first where one stands before it, with one value at each site, its sites in ascending order of their columns, and the fields of a record in ascending order, where the mask after its last site admits a token that closes it.
    - The atoms come in one order: at each place the tokens in ascending order of their ids, and an atom before the atoms that add sites to it.
    - The walk asks the checks once for each shape of an atom, and the atoms of one shape share the verdict. A shape holds the atom's relation, its columns and values, what stands on the stack, and whether the atom repeats a conjunct of the body. A label stands in a shape as its type, whether it is bound, whether a key marks it and whether an earlier site of the atom holds it. It stands as its id, with the verdicts held for that form alone, where a conjunct of the body gives it no type or where a type rule of the relation reads whether two labels stand in one grid.
    - In a body of an `INIT` form, what stands on the stack holds the component, the position of the input and the form's other bodies, other than the key body that a keyed input reads, whose labels have shapes. A label of another body stands as its id.
    - The verdicts of lib and fact relations read no program and no task, so the sessions of a process share them, other than a place whose closing token is no relation's. Those of a program's relations stand with the state's finished forms. `arclog.atom_verdicts` gives the shared verdicts as bytes, and `arclog.add_atom_verdicts` adds them in a process of the same build, so that its walks start warm. It refuses the bytes of another build, whose checks may differ. A verdict's key holds the settings that a check reads, so one file holds the verdicts of several settings.
    - `candidate_atoms_reference` gives the same list from a whole mask at each token, for a check of the walk.
- `atom_tables` in `arclog_tables` gives the summary of the table of each atom: the body in progress with that atom as a closed conjunct, at each assignment.
    - The body in progress of an `INIT` form is its key body, or an input's body after the key body's conjuncts where the input is keyed, with each label that the key marks as a label of the body. Its side is the side of the form with what it reads so far. Where the engine has named a body and no token has opened it, the body in progress is that body.
    - The body in progress in a branch of an `OR` is a body of its own: the conjuncts around the `OR`, then the conjuncts of the branch so far, with the branch's labels as columns. It reads no other branch.
    - A table holds the columns of the body, then the labels that the atom adds, in the order of its sites. Its features read that order: `suit` reads the first columns of a type.
    - Atoms with equal tables share one summary, and an atom holds the place of its summary. A summary holds the features at each assignment, and a hash of the rows at each assignment ([`Relation::rows_hash`](../../engine/arclog/src/relations.rs)), which tells equal rows at the same columns. It is no `TableHash`, and a program can hold the same columns in another order, so it compares the assignments of one atom alone.
    - An atom has no summary where no clause, `CHOOSE` or `INIT` body is in progress, where the check refuses the closed body, or where the atom's work passes its cap. The result lists the atoms whose work passed the cap.
    - The rows of an atom come from a join of its relation to the rows of the parts of the body that it reads, with no program made for the atom. An atom that this join does not read takes a program of its own: an atom in an `IF`, an `ALL` or a `NOT` of a form, a site of a record, a column with no fixed scalar type, a type rule that reads whether two labels stand in one grid, a read of a `DET` that the body does not read, a read that changes the side of an `INIT` form, and a relation per example read from a body of the task.
    - An atom that reads one part of the body's rows, at values that satisfy a mode of its relation, reads that part through groups: the rows with equal values at the atom's bound columns. The atom reads its relation once for each group, and the atoms that fill the same sites from the same groups share what they read, an atom and its `NOT` among them. The hash of the atom's table comes from sums over the groups, and the rows of a table are made where no earlier atom has that hash.
    - The session holds the result of the call by the state and its list of atoms, up to a bound on its bytes, since a search reaches a state of a shared prefix many times.
    - No deadline applies, and the summary holds no `DET`s of an assignment.

## Open parameters

- A `DET` with `(ONE)` in a synthesized program is an open parameter, under the readings of [Open values](cases.md#open-values) that a program with one case and no alt can hold: a `DET` whose body ends in `(ONE)`, whose models are its open values, and a `DET` by a proposer with `(ONE)`, whose open values the consistent assignments give.
- A `DET` without `(ONE)` in a candidate program is an open parameter of that second reading, at an `OUT` form's end, where it takes more than one value over the consistent assignments. A solver's author can pin such a value, and a candidate has no author, so under open-world candidates the values that the train pairs leave stay open.
- With several `DET`s of the second reading, the open values of each are the projection onto it of the consistent assignments, and the program runs over the consistent assignments, which are joint, and not over the product of the `DET`s' domains. A solver's open values stay one set per parameter, as [Models](cases.md#models) gives; no solver holds two open `DET`s with a body, so the joint reading differs from the product for a decoded program alone.
- The program reads as M_open over those values, as [Open parameters](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#open-parameters) gives: on an input, its output is the union of the grids its assignments give, and it is undefined where it is undefined at one of them.
- The open values are models of the case, as [Models](cases.md#models) gives, so M_open reads the output set of the `OUT` form at each consistent assignment, at the form's columns. The test at each assignment alone serves the filter of [`DET`](determination.md#det).
- At an `OUT` form's end, the form is consistent where M_open solves every train pair, as [Solves](cases.md#solves) gives for the run's candidates: under closed-world candidates the union is one candidate of the pair, and under open-world candidates it holds at most two grids, one of them the given output. On Kaggle, M_open's grids on a test input are its attempts, as [Solves](cases.md#solves) gives.
- The consistent assignments are evaluated already, as [Decode interface](#decode-interface) gives, so M_open over them reads their output sets and runs no evaluation of its own. M_open adds the evaluation of each consistent assignment on the test inputs, under the assignment cap of [Decode settings](#decode-settings).

## Factorized bodies

- A conjunction of atoms, a whole body or a part of one, has an entry in the result cache, keyed by its content. That includes partial atoms. Each entry is evaluated from its own reads by one join plan. Equal entries reuse their value, and other cached bodies do not select a different join for it.
- The cache keeps the parts of each body, so that a search that adds or removes any atom finds its entries. A part is a connected component of the body's atoms, where two atoms are connected when they share a variable. Each part has its own entry, and the body's rows are the product of its parts' rows.
- A part's entry holds its rows in two parts where a Position column splits its atoms: the atoms of one set read the column only at columns of algebra relations that read a Position through its frame alone, such as `box_extent`'s `P`, and the other set binds it. Where the values the other set gives the column have one frame, the first set is joined once, at one of them, and the entry's rows are the product of the two sets' rows. The row cap counts the rows that each of the two sets holds, and no row of the product is built.
- A part is a subset of the body's atoms, and not a prefix. So the same entries serve decoding, which adds each atom after the last, and a search that adds or removes any atom.
- A finished clause's entry is the entry of its whole body, whatever order built it. The join order of a run with no decode is the evaluator's, and every order gives the same rows.
- The count of a body's rows is the product of its parts' counts, and the body is empty when any part is empty. So pruning a decode on a count or on emptiness builds no product.
- A read that walks the product of a relation of more than one part, such as the domain of a `DET`, the input of an instance, the lifted form of a relation per example or the union of a relation's forms, counts each row of the product as a binding tried, before it builds any of them, against the binding cap of [Decode settings](#decode-settings). The index of an atom over the relation counts the combinations of the parts that hold its columns, each part read by its distinct projections onto those columns. A bounded observation also charges its own work budget. The join of each part counted that part's rows, and no join counts their product, so a product past a cap stops the read, and builds no row. A prune reads the positive atoms at the top level of the body and the complete forms alone, as [Programs as values](reader.md#programs-as-values) gives.
- The generic join of [Joins](evaluator.md#joins) does the last join from the parts' indexes.
- A negated atom whose variables lie in two parts links those parts, and it is checked when they are joined.

## Traces

- The engine keeps a trace of each decode and of each run: an append-only list of records, one per step, in the order of the steps.
    - In a decode, a step is one token. Its record holds the token; whether the language model or the engine emitted it; what the engine computed at that step, such as the rows and counts the caller asked for, the consistency test at an `OUT` form's end and, under the rule tests, the verdict at a `RULE` clause's end; and, where the caller attaches them, the language model's top-k tokens with their probabilities. The record also holds the count of the output sets that the step's tests ended at the model cap, `capped_sets`. Python reads the sum of the counts as `Decoder.capped_sets`, the count of one test as the `capped_sets` of its `Consistency`, and the count of the attempts as the second value of `Decoder.attempts_counted()`.
    - In a run, a step is the evaluation of one relation of one case on one input. Its record holds the row count, the models enumerated, the values determined, and a stop at a cap or a deadline.
- Three layers are expected to change as the synthesis work iterates: the observations given to the language model at a step, the rendering of the trace, and the layout of the vocabulary, of which the tokens of [Token stream](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#token-stream) are the starting layout. Each sits over the trace, and no part of the evaluator depends on it.
- A renderer is one such layer. The form of [Trace form](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#trace-form) is one renderer. The program form is the trace form under the rewrites of [From trace form to program form](../../docs/engine/arclog-stream.md#from-trace-form-to-program-form). The synthesis code decides which top-k tokens the trace form shows and how it shows a decision, and those choices are the renderer's.

## Shipping

- maturin builds `arclog-py` as an abi3 wheel for Python 3.12 and later, which covers the local 3.12 and Kaggle's 3.13. The probe under `work/kaggle-probe/` installed such a wheel offline. The wheel ships in a Kaggle dataset.
- The notebook loads the task, runs the program, and gives at most two attempts per test input, under a deadline per candidate program with cancellation, as the deadline of [Decode settings](#decode-settings) gives.
- A decoded program carries no stored value. Its run determines each `DET` from the evidence, as every determination is, and a run in the task session of its decode reuses that session's entries.
