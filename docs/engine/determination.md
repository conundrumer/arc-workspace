# Determination

It builds the derivation of each stored parameter from the observed data, by `DET`, the proposers and `RIGID_DET`. It rests on [rigid](../../docs/arclog/rigid.md) for `RIGID_DET`.

The step of the [build order](overview.md#build-order) that builds it ends when the determined values match the stored `param` records, as [End check](#end-check) gives, for each parameter that is not a pick and that no case with a `DEMONSTRATED` form reads, or the difference is reported as a fault, as the Principles requirement of [Requirements](overview.md#requirements) gives. The other parameters are checked in [Every case](cases.md#every-case).

## `DET`

- The `DET` relations are determined in dependency order.
- A `DET` whose body names no proposer is a determination by the solver's clauses: its rows are its value, and the train pairs filter none of them, as [determination](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#models-and-tasks) gives that kind. At the stored `param` records, such a `DET` that holds no `ONE` mark takes its rows from its clause too, a witness's `assign` gives it its rows in place of its clause's, and its record is compared only at [End check](#end-check).
- A `DET` with no body and no proposer, `(DET &x)` or `(DET &x (ONE))`, gives no value. Such a form is a fault of the conversion, as each one under the converter's `TODO no-det-plan` comment is, and the engine reports it. Its fix states a body that gives the `DET`'s domain. Where each open value is a set of rows, as each order of the colors in b7f8a4d8's `&draw_rank` and each order of the corners in bc93ec48's `&precedence` is, the body ends in `ONE` marks over the columns of those rows, as [One](../../docs/arclog/constraints.md#one) gives: over a row for each color and each rank, `(ONE Col)` and `(ONE R)` give each order as one model.
- A `DET` with no body by a proposer of one column has a domain of one value per row of the proposer's rows over the task, each value one row, as [Program](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#program) gives, unless the form ends in `(SET)`: then its domain is one value, the proposer's whole set of rows. A `DET` by a proposer of several columns that the registry does not mark `domain` has a domain of one value, the proposer's whole set of rows. A parameter whose value holds several rows and whose `DET` names a type-domain proposer is a fault of its solver.
- A `DET` with no body that names several proposers is a fault, since a `DET` names at most one, as [Program](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#program) gives, and the check refuses it. A `DET_OR_RULE` line with several proposers and no body has a stored reading per proposer, as [Stored readings](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#stored-readings) gives, each a `DET` by its proposer alone.
- A `DET` whose domain holds several values is filtered by the train pairs, as [determination](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#models-and-tasks) gives. The engine enumerates the assignments of these `DET` relations lazily, in dependency order, and a `DET` that a case takes from the concept program depends on each `DET` it reads there. It runs each case that reads them on the train pairs at each assignment, as [Determination with no stored value](#determination-with-no-stored-value) gives, and keeps, per case, the assignments at which that case solves every train pair. A train pair with two candidates is solved where the case solves either of them, as [Solves](cases.md#solves) gives: the stored assignment is one function of the evidence, and the split task is chosen after it, as [Cases](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#cases) gives Fit. `ex_output` holds the pair's given output.
- The filter runs on the cases that take, at every `DET_OR_RULE` and `RULE_OR_DET` line, the concept case's reading, or another stored reading of a line whose concept reading is stored, one per [base](cases.md#cases) and stored reading, less the cases that an `exclude` form leaves out. A stored reading by each proposer of a line with several determines the relation by that proposer alone, so the train pairs of its own case filter its values. A case that takes another reading at such a line reads its base's values of the other parameters, and the train pairs filter no value under it alone, as [Params](../../docs/handbook/solver-file.md#params) gives.
- Where several of those cases read a parameter, the engine projects each case's set of kept joint assignments onto the parameters they share, intersects the projections, and restricts each case's set to the intersection, as [Parameters across cases](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#parameters-across-cases) gives. A `DET` that is part of the determination, as [End check](#end-check) gives, takes no part in the intersection, so its value may differ across cases. A parameter is judged underdetermined after that restriction. A case that keeps no assignment or faults takes no part in the intersection. An evaluation stop rejects its path; the walk continues at the next value and keeps its first stop. A case with kept assignments is solved. With none, it is stopped where a stop occurred and unsolved otherwise.
- A case's open values of a parameter are those that [Params](../../docs/handbook/solver-file.md#params) gives: the values under the case that takes, at every line, the reading of its own case where that is a stored reading the filter runs on, and the concept case's reading otherwise.
- A parameter without `(ONE)` whose value differs across the assignments that are left is underdetermined, and the engine reports it as an error, where some input of the evidence, a train input or a test input, separates those assignments: a case that reads the parameter gives different outputs on that input at two of them, or has no output at one of them. No test output is read. With `(ONE)`, those values are its open values, as [Open values](cases.md#open-values) gives.
- Where no input of the evidence separates those assignments, the parameter is not underdetermined, and M_open over its values gives the one output on each input. This says that no input of the task separates the values. It does not say that they agree, as [Equivalence and subsumption](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#equivalence-and-subsumption) defines agreement, and a witness can separate them.
- The product of the `DET`s' domains runs under a cap of 256, which is the default of the assignment cap of [Decode settings](synthesis.md#decode-settings). The product is over the domain of each `DET` that the relation being evaluated reads, task-wide. The cap applies to a solver's run, to each prefix of a decode, and to a run on Kaggle. Its evidence is the largest product of the base cases of the 939 solvers of the corpus's 953 whose determination ended in the run of [End check](#end-check), which `Caps::CORPUS` in `engine/arclog/src/eval/values.rs` gives with its solver, and the cap is the least power of two above twice that product. A product past the cap is an alert that names the parameters, as [Bounds](overview.md#requirements) gives. The model cap of [The constraint module](cases.md#the-constraint-module) is a cap of its own, per case and per input.

## Determination with no stored value

- A decoded program carries no stored value, and the determination reads none either: each `DET` is determined from the evidence, and the run reads the determined values. The `param` records are records of a run and not its source, as [Params](../../docs/arclog/arcdata.md#params) gives, so [End check](#end-check) compares the determined values with them.
- The meaning is one evaluation of the case per assignment, through the result cache: an entry that reads no `DET` whose domain holds several values is shared by every assignment, and an entry that reads one is computed per assignment. Under an assignment, each form reads as at one stored value:
    - `NOT`, `ALL` and an `IF` test read the relation under that assignment, so a negated atom holds under each assignment at which the atom has no row.
    - An instance's inputs are taken per assignment, and a construction runs once per assignment over them, so its outputs are per assignment.
    - The models of a `ONE` or `CHOOSE` pick, and of a `DEMONSTRATED` form, are built per input and per assignment, and the train pairs filter no model. A train pair is solved under an assignment only where its whole output set over the models is one of its candidates, as [Solves](cases.md#solves) gives.
    - The read of the `OUT` form runs per assignment, as [The output of the `OUT` form](cases.md#the-output-of-the-out-form) gives.
- An assignment is dropped where an evaluation reaches a bound or the consistency test, the filter of [`DET`](#det) at the train pairs, rejects it. An empty relation under an assignment drops nothing, since a later `NOT` can read the empty relation.
- A tagged form of that evaluation holds the rows of every assignment in one relation, each row with the assignment it was derived under in an added column. A row derived from no such `DET` holds for every assignment, as the `nil` of the Souffle `OpenValue` column does. Where a body reads several such `DET`s, a row's assignment gives a value to each, and a join keeps the rows whose assignments agree on each `DET` that both sides read. A negated atom holds at a row under each of the row's assignments at which it has no row. The tagged form gives the rows of the evaluation per assignment. It is provisional: no measurement compares its cost with that of one evaluation per assignment.

## Proposers

- A proposer written in arclog has its clause in `engine/lib/proposers.arclog`. Over the evidence it runs on, it gives the relation of a `DET` whose body is that clause, at the columns its `(columns ...)` form names in `engine/lib/registry.sexpr`.
- On the derived reading of a `DET_OR_RULE` or `RULE_OR_DET` line, the proposer runs on each input, a pair's or a witness's, alone, with `ex_output` empty. Where it gives no value, each case that takes that reading is undefined on that input, as the `@derive` line of [Params](../../docs/handbook/solver-file.md#params) gives. Where it gives several values and the line has no `(ONE)`, the parameter takes those values, as that line gives too.
- A type-domain proposer, as [Program](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#program) gives it, is native: it enumerates the values of its type that the evidence gives, and reads no stored value. The evidence is the train inputs and outputs and the test inputs.
    - An orientation type: every value of the type.
    - Color: the colors of the grids of the evidence.
    - Number: 0 up to the largest extent of a grid of the evidence.
    - A pair type: the product of the values of its two types.
    - Box, Offset and Position have no type-domain proposer. No frame bounds a Box or an Offset, and a cell of the largest frame of the evidence is never a cell of a frame of tiles, as 7fe24cdd's `&anchor` and ed74f2f2's `&target_tile` are; the Souffle tooling took those cells from the frame of the stored record, which a determination from the evidence does not have. A `DET` of one of these types states a body that gives its domain.
- Each other proposer whose derivation needs a form that a program bans, such as arithmetic, and that no lib relation gives, is native too, with its columns in the registry.
- The fields of a frame of cells or of a frame cut into equal tiles come from `DET`s that the program states, as [Program](../../docs/arclog/arclog.md#program) gives. A converter's form that stores the fields, `(DET &x Frame)` or `(DET &x Division)`, is a fault of the conversion.

## `RIGID_DET`

- The engine computes `RIGID_DET` by the method of [Implementation](../../docs/engine/rigid.md#implementation). The frame of each assembly is the reading of its instance whose reading is least by content, in the canonical order of terms, so the frame depends on no order of encodings.
- An assembly's instances are those its `instance` clause gives, and a form with none has one instance, as [rigid](../../docs/arclog/rigid.md) gives. The engine infers no instance from a part's columns: a part column the `instance` clause does not name is a column of the part, and a part that reads no label of the `instance` clause is a frame part, which no instance reads: at an assignment its rows are those its `DET` takes, in the frame of the assembly.
- No output observes that choice, as [Invariance](../../docs/handbook/principles.md#invariance) requires: the checker gives each `:Pose` column the type Pose, and refuses every read that would see the frame, as [The `RIGID_DET` frame](equivariance.md#the-rigid_det-frame) gives, and the assembly's own Idents, those of its key included, are as unobservable as the pose, as [The pose](../../docs/arclog/rigid.md#the-pose) gives.
- A record of an assembly's parts was written in Souffle's frame, so the parts compare with the records up to one D4 element, the same element for every part of the assembly, and, through the renaming of Idents, one renaming. Under a `key` clause each key is an assembly of its own, with its own element. A part's column at a key label holds that key column again, and a record holds it once.
- A witness's `assign` of `&<assembly>.<part>` is the output's rows, whatever the form's clauses are: the instances give no frame to place and no pose to match for it, and the output holds the assigned rows.
- In a form with no `instance` clause, a part whose `DET` has no record, no `assign` and no clause of its own is a frame part at the stored records.
- At the stored records, a frame part whose `DET` has no record or `assign` of its own takes the record of `&<assembly>.<part>`, imaged onto the frame by each element that takes the records of the other parts onto the frame. Where no element does, or two that do give the record different images, the assembly has no value and each of its outputs is `undefined`.

## End check

- `arclog-cli determine <corpus-dir> <tasks-dir> [<task_id> ...]` determines the parameters of each solver given, or of every solver of the corpus, from its task's evidence, and compares each determined value with the solver's `param` rows. It prints each difference by solver and parameter. For a parameter with several values that no input of the evidence separates, as [`DET`](#det) gives, the record is compared with each of the values, and the check passes where it is one of them. A stored run uses the record, and so does a run at the determined values. The check prints each such parameter, and the count of the solvers that pass with one.
- A `DET` that a case's `OUT` form reads, through clauses, instances and `RIGID_DET` assemblies and through no `DET`'s body, is a parameter, and a parameter with no `param` row is a fault. A `DET` by its clauses that reads no fact of the task, other than through a `DET` it reads, is computed from those `DET`s, and it isn't a parameter. Each `DET` that isn't a parameter is part of the determination, and needs no row.
- Idents compare up to one renaming, as [Parallelism and determinism](evaluator.md#parallelism-and-determinism) gives, and the parts of a `RIGID_DET` assembly as [`RIGID_DET`](#rigid_det) gives. A `DET` with no body and no proposer, and each other fault of a solver or of the conversion that this doc names, is reported as that fault, and is not a difference. A parameter that no `param` record gives is reported as a fault, other than a part `DET` of a `RIGID_DET` assembly whose parts the records give.

## How each decision can fail

| Decision | Failure | What catches or bounds it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Determination filtered by the train pairs over a product | many `DET`s whose domains hold several values | dependency order with shared entries, and a cap that reports the parameters |
| The tagged form of the evaluation per assignment | its rows differ from those of one evaluation per assignment, at a negation or a construction | the end check, which reads the determined values |
| The Pose type | a read that sees no frame and that the rule refuses | `pose_difference` gives every relation between two poses; every clause of `gen/` that reads a pose passes the rule |
