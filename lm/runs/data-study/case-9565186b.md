## What I see

The task: in a 3x3 grid, the color with the most cells keeps its cells, and every other cell becomes 5. Each table has one assignment. The case also holds the tables of the sibling cases: `$BlobColor` and `$Blob` choose by the largest 4-component, and the output of `$Count` reads none of their tables.

- **&r0** (Cardinality: cells per color): 3, 4, 2 and 4 rows. In example 0, color 2 has 5 cells, 8 has 3 and 1 has 1. This is the first table that adds signal: a count per color, keyed by color.
- **&r3** (Connectivity: 4-components): 9 rows in each example, one per cell. Without its Ident column, the drawing is the input grid. The only signal it adds is in c2: which cells share a component. In example 3 the two 8s, (0,2) and (2,0), get id10 and id9. id5 is the cell set {(2,1), (2,2)} in example 1 (color 2) and also in example 3 (color 1).
- **&r7** (cells per component): its rows equal &r0's in examples 0 to 2. In example 3 it has 5 rows to &r0's 4, because color 8 splits into id9 and id10, at 1 cell each. That one difference is the only signal &r7 adds over &r0.
- **&r10** (DET AnyOutNewColor): 5, the one color that the outputs have and the inputs do not. It is read from the outputs and has one row.
- **&r11, &r12** (Order of &r0: rank up, rank down): each is a function of &r0's counts. They add no signal, and no later clause reads them.
- **&r13** (the least colors): 1, 1, 1 and 3 rows. Example 3's 3 rows (1, 3 and 8, all at count 2) are a tie. The row count varies over the examples where the other examples hold one row, and this shows a tie that the program must break or must not use.
- **&r14** (the most color): one row in each example: 2, 1, 2, 4. Here the signal about the rule jumps. Many rows reduce to one color per example, and no example has a tie.
- **&r15, &r16** (Order's pairs over &r0): &r15 is the pairs of adjacent ranks (example 0: `1 | 8`, `8 | 2`). &r16 is another pair relation that I could not name from its rows: in example 0 it adds `2 | 1`, which closes a cycle, and in example 3 it is &r15 plus &r15 reversed. Both follow from &r0's counts, so both add no signal.
- **&r21** (input cells of &r14's color): 5, 4, 7 and 3 cells. In each example the drawing is the set of output cells that keep their input color. It is a selection that matches the output.
- **&r22 to &r27** (Order over &r7's components): the same forms as &r11 to &r16, over components. &r24 (the least) has 2 rows in example 3, id9 and id10, a tie of two components of one cell. **&r25** (the largest component) is id2, id4, id7 and id12. Its colors 2, 1, 2 and 4 equal &r14's at every example, and **&r32** restates &r25.
- **&r33** (the output): &r21's cells, and 5 at every other cell. It equals the output at every train example.

Rows that look like noise: the chain &r3, &r7, &r22 to &r27 and &r32, which the output of `$Count` does not read; &r11, &r12, &r15 and &r16, which are orders as ranks or pairs that nothing reads. &r25 against &r14 is noise of another kind: two tables from two hypotheses (most cells per color; largest component) agree at every train example, so the train data cannot tell the cases apart. The test input does not tell them apart either: color 3 has the most cells (4) and the largest component.
