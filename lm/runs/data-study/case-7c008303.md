## What I see

The task: a cross of 8s cuts the 9x9 grid into a 2x2 key, a 6x6 pattern and two blank quadrants. The output is the pattern, cut into one 3x3 block per key cell, with each 3 recolored to the key cell of its block. Each table has one assignment.

Many Boxes in this case are positions in a frame other than the 9x9 grid, and the renderer prints them as boxes of the input grid. `r0-7 c0-7` is tile (0,0) of the 2x2 frame of quadrants (margins 0 1 0 1). `r0-7 c0-3` in &r22 is cell (0,0) of a 2x6 quadrant. `(0,0)out` in &r25 is a cell of the 6x6 pattern frame, and it prints as an output cell only because the output is also 6x6.

- **&r0** (DominantColor): 0.
- **&r1** (Singleton: lines of one color): 2 rows in each example, the divider row and the divider column, both 8. It is a selection of 2 of 18 lines, and the first table that adds signal. The row `r2-2 c0-8` has AXIS_V, and the table alone does not tell whether an axis names the line's direction or its normal.
- **&r2** (lines of more than one color) and **&r3** (line-color pairs, 54 rows): the complement of &r1, and the input regrouped by line. No later clause reads them.
- **&r4** (&r1 without color 0): equal to &r1, a restatement.
- **&r5** (cells of a divider, with the two directions across it): 36 rows. The drawing is the cross, with 4 at the crossing. It is a product of &r4's cells with two directions.
- **&r6 to &r15** (Order over margins, DividerTiles's cut): **&r8**, **&r9** and **&r12** are equal: each divider's margin from each edge. The values are always 2 and 6, and they sit under other keys in each example: example 0 T2 B6 L2 R6, example 1 T2 B6 L6 R2, example 2 T6 B2 L2 R6. The signal is where the cross sits, and it is in how the keys get the values, since each example has the same value multiset. &r11 is &r8 with its Number column repeated, &r10 is empty, and &r14 and &r15 are the directions times 0 to 8, a product with the grid's range. In &r7, c2 is 1 at the distances short of the divider.
- **&r16, &r17** (288 rows: each cell's distance and side toward each edge): a product of the cells with directions. Over &r8 they add only which side of the cross each cell lies on.
- **&r18** (128 rows: each cell off the cross with its quadrant): the drawing is 2 at each cell off the cross and `.` on the cross. This is a function from a cell to a tile, a partition of the grid.
- **&r19** (Bbox of each quadrant): 4 rows. In example 0: tile (0,0) is `r0-1 c0-1` (2x2), (0,1) is `r0-1 c3-8` (2x6), (1,0) is `r3-8 c0-1` (6x2), (1,1) is `r3-8 c3-8` (6x6). This is the first table that holds the quadrant sizes.
- **&r21** (128 rows: the extent of each cell's quadrant on each axis): a product of &r19's extents with &r18's cells. It adds no signal.
- **&r22** (64 rows: each cell with its color, quadrant, quadrant box and position inside its quadrant): a restatement of the input in the quadrant frame.
- **&r23** (non-0 colors per quadrant): 5, 4 and 5 rows. In example 0, tile (0,0) has 1, 2, 4 and 6, tile (1,1) has 3, and the blank quadrants have no rows. Here the signal about the rule jumps: one quadrant holds the key's colors, one holds the pattern's single color, and two hold nothing.
- **&r24** (key and pattern: two filled quadrants, extent 2 against 6): 2 rows, one per axis. Many rows reduce to one pair per example: example 0 has key (0,0) and pattern (1,1), and example 2 has key (1,0) and pattern (0,1). The ratio 6 / 2 = 3, the block side, is in the rows but in no single column.
- **&r25** (Division: each pattern cell, its block and its position in the block): 36 rows, one per cell of the 6x6 frame. **&r26** is its cells, and **&r27** is the block count, 2 on each axis in every example: a constant.
- **&r30** (the output): 36 rows. c4 is the output cell, c1 the pattern color and c2 the output color. Pattern color 0 goes to 0. Pattern color 3 goes to the key color of the cell's block: in example 0, `(3,1)out` goes to 1 and `(0,1)out` goes to 2, as in the output. The rows equal the output at every train example, but the renderer draws a table by its first Box column, which here is the key's quadrant, so the match is not drawn.

Rows that look like noise: &r2, &r3 and &r4; &r9 to &r12, &r14 and &r15 (restatements or a product with the grid's range); &r16, &r17 and &r21 (products of cells with directions or extents); &r22 (the input in another frame). Most of the rows of this case lie in tables that add no signal: 288 + 288 + 128 + 128 of the about 1,290 rows of a train example.
